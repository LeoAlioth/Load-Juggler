# Release Notes

## 2.1.4

### Bug Fixes

- **A hot water tank can be driven through a water heater**: the tank's thermostat could only be a `climate` entity, so a device that Home Assistant exposes as a `water_heater` - a Vaillant boiler's hot water, for example - could not be picked at all. It can now. A water heater is gated by its target temperature alone: the tank's setpoint while it may heat, the water heater's lowest target while it may not, so a tank colder than that still heats. It is never switched off - on a Mitsubishi heat pump through MELCloud, switching the water heater off powers down the whole unit, space heating included - and its operation mode is never changed (they are each integration's own: the Vaillant offers *heating*, *hot water only* and *stand by*). The target is written only when it differs from the one already set, as MELCloud and other cloud services limit how often a device can be written. A water heater does not say whether it is heating, which is how a tank frees its power for other loads once the water is hot: with the tank's power sensor configured, above 10 W it is heating and below it the power is freed, exactly as a thermostat reporting *idle* does. Without one it is taken as heating, at its *Heating element power*, while the tank is colder than the water heater's own target, and as satisfied once it is not - the way a thermostat's *heating* and *idle* are read. That matters for boosting: the rating is added back to the export the Excess verdict reads, so the tank's own draw is not mistaken for the house eating the surplus that started the boost. On a heat pump without a meter (1.5 kW drawn, 6.9 kW still exported) the verdict released at the first extra watt, a minute after the boost began. Set the element power to what the heater draws while heating; a heater's own restart hysteresis still reads as heating, which books power that is not drawn rather than the reverse. Tanks on a `climate` entity are unchanged.
- **A load's sliders now follow its settings page**: every load slider - a tank's *Away*, *Normal* and *Boost* temperatures and its element power, a plug's power, a charger's minimum and maximum current, a power station's charge power and reserve - starts from the value on the load's settings page, but once it had a state of its own that state won for ever. A value changed on the settings page afterwards, or entered for a load re-created under the same name, never reached the slider: a tank set up with 40, 42 and 60 °C kept the sliders it had before. The slider now remembers which setting it started from, and when the setting has changed since, the new setting wins; a slider moved by hand keeps its value for as long as the setting stays put. A slider already out of step with its setting before updating keeps its value, since nothing recorded which setting it started from; set it once by hand, and from then on it follows the settings page.

## 2.1.3

### Bug Fixes

- **Off-grid with inverter output sensors and *Parallel* wiring, the battery's discharge is no longer published as solar**: with no grid, an inverter's output is everything the site draws from it - solar plus the battery's discharge, less its charging - whichever wiring is chosen. On *Series* wiring Load Juggler took the battery's power off the output to find the solar production; on *Parallel* - the setup form's default - it took the whole output as solar. At night on a 6 kW inverter with the car charging, the Solar Power sensor read **5992 W**, all of it from the battery, and by day it read the same 5992 W with 3000 W from the panels; the same figure went into long-term statistics, the per-inverter solar sensor, the Overview and the forecast accuracy measurement. It also reached charging: with the battery above its SOC target, a *Solar Only* or *Solar Priority* load may use the battery's spare discharge, bounded by what the inverter has left, and with the discharge counted once as solar and again as discharge, the inverter's output was set against its rating twice: past half the rating the battery had nothing more to offer, and the load was held at whatever it was drawing - in a closed-loop test a Solar Only car settled at **12.4 A** at night (18.0 A by day) where *Series* wiring gave it **21.7 A**. Off-grid both wirings now take the battery's power off the output: the sensor reads 0 W at night and 3000 W by day, and the Solar Only car gets 21.7 A on either wiring. Without a battery power sensor nothing tells the panels' share from the battery's - see the next entry. Grid-tied sites are not affected.
- **Off-grid with a battery whose power is not read, the inverter's whole output is no longer published as solar**: with inverter output sensors but no battery power reading (no battery power sensor, or one unreadable past its hold), nothing tells the panels' share of the output from the battery's, and Load Juggler published the whole output as solar production - on either wiring, at night all of it battery: an inverter putting out 2300 W read **2300 W** on Current Solar Power and on the inverter's Solar Production sensor, went into long-term statistics and the Overview, and was learned from as production by the forecast accuracy, peakiness and clipped-energy measurements (with a full battery, 78 Wh of "clipped" energy booked in one minute against a 7 kW forecast). Both sensors now read unknown in that configuration, the Overview shows a dash, and the forecast measurements skip those intervals - as they now also do while a solar production sensor is unreadable with nothing to hold, which used to be learned from as 0 W. Charging is not affected: the calculation still sizes the chargers on the whole output, as before. An inverter with no battery configured still publishes its output as solar. Add a battery power sensor or a solar production sensor to see the production.
- **Off-grid with inverter output sensors, Solar Remaining Power no longer counts the house's share of the sun as spare**: Solar Remaining Power (and Solar Remaining Current) is meant to be what solar offers the managed loads - the production less what the house takes from it, as on a grid-tied site with a solar production sensor. Off-grid with inverter output sensors the house was taken off nothing, on either wiring, and the sensor read the whole production: with 3000 W of sun and a 1 kW house it read **3000 W** (13.0 A) where **2000 W** (8.7 A) is left, with the car off and charging alike. It now takes off the house the inverters are serving - the same figure Household Power shows - so it reads 2000 W. Display only: charging is sized as before.
- **Grid-tied, the inverter's output is no longer counted twice when the chargers are sized**: what a charger may be offered is the grid's import allowance plus solar plus the battery's usable discharge, less the house. Load Juggler splits that into what the grid can still give (the allowance less the import the meter would show with the managed loads off) and what the inverters can still give - and built the second half from *gross* production, solar plus the battery's discharge rating less the discharge already flowing. That only adds up when the solar figure is itself worked out from the meter's export, as on a site with no inverter output sensors and no solar sensor. Everywhere else the inverter's output went wrong, and it mattered wherever a *Max Import Power* or the main breaker binds: with a **solar production sensor**, or a PV inverter's **output sensor**, the full production was offered although the house was already using part of it - with 5 kW of sun and a 1 kW house under a 0 W import allowance a car was permitted 21.7 A (5 kW), and the site imported 1 kW past its limit; on a 25 A breaker two chargers took the site to 29.3 A. On a **Series hybrid read through its output sensor**, whose solar is worked out as output less battery flow, the discharge in flight carries the chargers' own draw, so a car running on the battery counted its own draw as spent: at a 0 W allowance a 3 kW car behind a 1 kW house on a 5 kW battery was cut after every start, and at 2 kW it settled at 12.9 A where 26.1 A was right. Once that battery reached its rating the grid carried the rest *through* the hybrid, its output sensor read the import too, and the import was offered again as solar: the car was permitted its full 32 A and the site imported 4.4 kW past a 2 kW limit. The inverters' half is now what they put out beyond the house - the export the meter would show with the managed loads off, plus the battery's discharge rating less its flow - the same quantity off-grid sites now use. In closed-loop tests through the real update cycle every case above settles on the allowance: 17.4 A and 26.1 A on the hybrid with no stops, 13.0 A on the battery at its rating, 17.4 A on the solar and output sensors, and the site never more than 3 W past its limit. Sites with no output or solar sensor were already right and are unchanged. (A battery whose power is not read is covered below.)
- **A symmetric inverter's output is no longer offered on a phase it cannot reach**: on a three-phase site with a symmetric inverter (one that puts out the same on every phase), the inverters' share was spread evenly across the phases, so a phase whose house took more than its share of the output was offered the other phases' export on top of its whole breaker allowance. With 15 A of house on one phase of a 6 kW array and a 25 A breaker, a car on that phase was permitted 24.5 A where 18.7 A was left, and the phase carried 30.8 A. Each phase is now offered its own export plus its share of the battery's spare discharge, and the car gets 18.7 A.
- **With *Allow Grid Charging* off, the export is no longer counted twice**: the grid's share was capped at what the site exports, but the export is the inverters' output, which their share already offers - with 5 kW of sun, a 1 kW house and a 2 kW battery a car was offered 32 A and imported 1.36 kW with grid charging off. With it off the grid now offers nothing and the car gets the 26.1 A the sun and the battery can carry.
- **The inverter's rating now leaves room for the house it already serves**: with an *Inverter Max Power* configured, what the inverters could still hand the chargers was capped at their full rating - but the output already serving the house is inside that rating. On a hybrid near its rating with a big house the chargers were offered the whole rating, the inverter clipped and the grid carried the difference: with 3 kW of sun and a 3 kW house on a 5 kW inverter, a car was permitted **21.7 A** where the inverter had 2 kW (8.7 A) left, and the site imported 3 kW past a 0 W import allowance (on a 20 A breaker the same car took 23.3 A through it). The cap is now the rating less the house's share - the solar and battery power that does not leave as export, or on a *Series* hybrid with output sensors the house behind it less what the grid carries - and the car gets 8.7 A (17.4 A with a 2 kW allowance, 28.7 A on the 20 A breaker). Without a solar production sensor or inverter output sensors the sun the house uses itself reaches no meter, so only the battery's share is taken off: the same car gets 13.0 A there, 1 kW over, where it got 21.7 A.
- **Without a solar production sensor or inverter output sensors, the inverter's rating no longer counts a charger's own supply as house load**: this fixes a regression from the rating cap above, earlier in this release. On such a site the solar is worked out from the meter - the export with the managed loads' draw handed back, plus what the battery is charging - and that export carries the battery's discharge feeding the car, so the house's share of the inverter came out as the battery's whole discharge, the car's own share included. With a 1 kW house, a 5 kW battery and a 0 W import allowance, a car the battery carried at night at 17.4 A under a 6 kW rating was **cut to 0 A** and restarted every few minutes (at 13.0 A under a 4 kW rating the same), with a 2 kW allowance it settled at **15.3 A** where 26.1 A fits, and by day with 3 kW of sun on an 8 kW inverter at **21.6 A** where 30.4 A fits. The house's share is now only what the battery puts out beyond that export - the same correction Current Solar Power got - and the car charges steadily at 17.4 A, 13.0 A, 26.1 A and 30.4 A. The sun the house uses itself still reaches no meter on such a site, so by day it is not taken off the rating: with 3 kW of sun and a 3 kW house on a 5 kW inverter the car is permitted 21.7 A again and the grid carries 3 kW past a 0 W allowance, as before the rating cap - this replaces the note above that it gets 13.0 A there. Sites with a solar production sensor or inverter output sensors are unchanged, and so is one whose battery power is not read.
- **Without a solar production sensor or inverter output sensors, a hybrid at its rating is no longer asked for more**: on such a site the sun the house uses itself reaches no meter, so by day the rating cap above cannot take it off, and the chargers were offered the battery's discharge rating although the inverter had no room left to pass it. With 3 kW of sun, a 1 kW house, a 7 kW hybrid and a 5 kW battery under a 0 W import allowance, a car was permitted **30.4 A** where 26.1 A fits; the inverter clipped, the battery sat at 4 kW, 1 kW below its rating, and the grid carried 1 kW past the allowance for as long as the car ran. A hybrid at its limit cannot answer more load, so the grid does: when the grid carries the chargers past what the import allowance leaves them, the import has risen with the chargers' own growth, and the battery - below its rating - has not risen with it, for a minute on end, Load Juggler now holds the battery at the discharge it was giving and offers the chargers what the inverter already gives beyond the house, and the grid's allowance as before. The car comes down to 26.4 A - within the permit's 0.3 A deadband of the 26.1 A that fits - and the grid carries it past the allowance only while that is being seen - about 2 minutes at the start and about 1.5 minutes each time the rating is offered again, 15 minutes after the verdict, then 30, then hourly. A battery its own logic holds back (its BMS, its own reserve or charge priority, a charge from the grid on its own schedule) looks the same from the meter and gives no more either, so it is held to the allowance too; where what is left is below the car's minimum the car stops, and is started again at each of those offers. A battery that answers the site within a minute, an import the chargers did not raise (the battery's own grid setpoint, the house), an import inside the allowance, and a battery below the *SOC Min* are left alone, and so is every site with a solar production sensor or inverter output sensors. Tested in closed loop against a simulated hybrid, not yet on a real site without those sensors.
- **Site Remaining Power now shows what the chargers are actually offered**: the figure, and Remaining Current A/B/C, Grid Remaining Power/Current and Inverter Remaining Current beside it, were worked out a second time from the site's headroom terms rather than read from the pool the chargers were sized from, and the two disagreed on almost half of the test scenarios. With *Allow Grid Charging* off it published the main breaker's headroom - up to **22 kW** - that the chargers were never offered; and wherever the inverters carried a charger it took the inverters' current output, the charger's own draw included, off their rating, so a car running on the battery behind a 0 W import allowance saw **2 W** remaining while it was being offered 4 kW. These sensors now read that pool itself (the same one the Overview's pool detail shows): Site Remaining Power is the grid's and the inverters' offer together, a charger's own draw included, and Remaining Current A/B/C is what a single-phase load on that phase could be offered - so where a site-wide limit binds (an import allowance, the inverter's rating) the three no longer add up to the total, because each phase can have it but not all three at once. Battery Remaining Power is unchanged; for Solar Remaining Power see the next entry. Entity IDs are unchanged.
- **Solar Remaining Power now shows the sun the chargers are actually offered**: Solar Remaining Power (and Solar Remaining Current) was worked out a second time as the solar production less the house, and the house was only known beside a solar production sensor or, off-grid, inverter output sensors. Everywhere else nothing was taken off and the whole solar figure was published: grid-tied with inverter output sensors, 3 kW of sun and a 1 kW house read **3000 W** (13.0 A) where 2000 W (8.7 A) is left, on a PV inverter and a *Series* hybrid alike; with no output or solar sensor the solar is worked out from the meter's export, which carries the battery's discharge, so a car the battery was carrying read as spare sun - **7 kW** by day and **4 kW** at night, where 2 kW and nothing were left. The sensors now read the sun's share of the pool *Solar Only* and *Solar Priority* loads are offered - the export with the managed loads off, plus what the sun is putting into the battery, less the discharge the export carries - and read 2000 W by day and 0 W at night on every one of these sites; with a solar production sensor they read as before. While the battery's power is not read nothing separates the sun from the battery's discharge, and the sensor reads unknown (see below). Off-grid with no battery it is the sun less the house (see below). Display only: charging is sized as before.
- **Off-grid with no inverter output sensors and a battery whose power is not read, Solar Remaining Power reads unknown**: on such a site solar plus battery power is the one measure of what it draws, and without the battery's half nothing measures the house - Load Juggler already offers the chargers nothing on the inverters' word there. Solar Remaining Power (and Solar Remaining Current) still published the whole solar figure, **3000 W** (13.0 A) with 3 kW on the solar sensor, as if the house took none of it. Both sensors now read unknown (and stay available) in that configuration, as Current Solar Power does where nothing splits an inverter's output from its battery, and the Overview shows a dash. Charging is not affected. Add a battery power sensor to see the figure.
- **Without a solar production sensor or inverter output sensors, the battery's discharge is no longer published as solar**: on such a site the solar is worked out from the meter - the export with the managed loads' draw handed back, plus what the battery is charging - and that export carries the battery's discharge too: a car the battery covers comes back as export. With 3 kW of sun, a 1 kW house and a 5 kW battery carrying the car, Current Solar Power read **6992 W** by day and **4000 W** at night, and Household Power, worked out from it, **4992 W** and **5000 W**. With the battery's power read, its discharge is now taken back off: Current Solar Power reads 2000 W by day and 0 W at night - the sun the house does not use itself, as the rest of it reaches no meter - and Household Power 0 W and 1000 W. With a battery whose power is not read (no battery power sensor, or one unreadable past its hold) nothing tells the sun from the battery in the export, and both sensors read unknown (and stay available), as off-grid where nothing splits an inverter's output from its battery; the Overview shows a dash. A site without a battery is unchanged. Display only: charging is not changed.
- **Grid-tied with a battery whose power is not read, Solar Remaining Power reads unknown**: Solar Remaining Power (and Solar Remaining Current) is the sun's share of what *Solar Only* and *Solar Priority* loads are offered - the export with the managed loads off, plus what the sun is putting into the battery, less the battery's discharge the export carries. With no battery power reading (no battery power sensor, or one unreadable past its hold) neither battery term is known and the figure was the bare export: at night, with a 2 kW import allowance starting the car and the battery covering it, it read **4000 W** (17.4 A) beside a solar sensor reading 0 W - every watt of it the battery's - and by day, the battery taking the sun the house leaves unseen, 0 W. Nothing tells the sun from the battery in the export there, so both sensors now read unknown (and stay available) and the Overview shows a dash. Display only: charging is not changed. This replaces the note above that the figure then includes whatever the battery exports.
- **Off-grid with inverter output sensors and a battery whose power is not read, Solar Remaining Power reads unknown**: there Current Solar Power already reads unknown, since nothing splits the inverter's output into sun and battery - but Solar Remaining Power (and Solar Remaining Current), which off-grid is built from the battery's flow, read **0 W** (0.0 A) on either wiring, a figure that measured nothing. Both now read unknown too (and stay available), and the Overview shows a dash. Display only: charging is not changed. Add a battery power sensor to see the figure.
- **Off-grid three-phase with a symmetric inverter, a single-phase load now gets what its phase can really deliver**: grid-tied, a symmetric inverter puts the same out on every phase, so a load on one phase gets a third of its extra output and the rest is exported. Off-grid there is nothing to export to - the inverter delivers exactly what each phase draws, from a battery and panels it shares across all three - but a single-phase load was still offered only a third of the battery's spare discharge. With 1.5 kW of house on a 4 kW battery, a car on one phase was offered 3.6 A where the battery could carry 10.9 A, under its 6 A minimum, and never started; a *Solar Only* car beside 2.5 kW of surplus was offered the same third. And with no per-phase rating configured nothing held a load that did start to its phase: a car on a 6 kW inverter (2 kW per phase) climbed to 19.6 A, 3 kW past that phase's leg. A single-phase load now gets the battery's whole headroom (and the surplus), as far as its phase can carry it - the configured per-phase rating, or a third of the inverter's rating on a symmetric inverter - and the inverter's total: 10.9 A, 6.5 A on the 2 kW leg. Three-phase loads and single-phase sites are unchanged.
- **Off-grid with no battery, the chargers get the sun the house leaves - and no more**: on an off-grid site with no battery at all (a PV-only off-grid inverter, or one with no battery configured) the sun is the whole supply, and what the managed loads can use is the production less the house, within the inverter's rating and each phase's leg. Load Juggler sized its off-grid offers from the battery's flow, and with no battery it took that flow as unread rather than as zero. The pool *Solar Only*, *Solar Priority* and *Excess* loads are sized from was left empty: with 4 kW of sun on a solar production sensor beside inverter output sensors and a 1 kW house, a *Solar Only* car was offered **0 A** and a *Solar Priority* or *Excess* car sat at its **6 A** minimum, where 13.0 A was spare, and Solar Remaining Power read 0 W. What a charger could be offered at all fell back to the whole solar figure, the house never taken off: a *Standard* car got 17.4 A where 13.0 A was left, a *Solar Priority* car ran its 6 A minimum where the house took all the sun (1.4 kW more than the panels gave), and read through output sensors alone a *Standard* car was handed the house's share again every cycle and climbed to 32 A, 7.4 kW past the sun. Every mode now gets the production less the house - 13.0 A there (8.7 A where a 3 kW inverter binds), nothing where the house takes all the sun - and Solar Remaining Power reads the 3000 W that is spare, not the 0 W noted above. Without a battery an off-grid array follows demand, so the sun nobody draws shows only on a solar production sensor read beside output sensors that measure the house. With output sensors alone, or a solar sensor alone, the production is the house plus the chargers, nothing is measured as spare, and a charger that is not already charging no longer starts - it used to, on the house's own share (with a solar sensor alone a *Standard* car got 17.4 A in 4 kW of sun behind a 1 kW house, and now gets nothing). Sites with a battery, and grid-tied sites, are unchanged.
- **Off-grid with no battery, read through inverter output sensors alone or a solar sensor alone, a load that is not running now starts by trying the sun**: on such a site the array produces only what is drawn, so nothing shows how much sun is spare, and since the fix above a load that was not already running never started. Load Juggler now tries: it offers the waiting load its minimum current and watches the production. If the production rises by that much, the sun had room - the load keeps it, and is offered one more step of its minimum the same way, for as long as the production keeps following. If it has not risen within the load's *Update frequency* plus 30 s (45 s by default: 15 s for the car to settle, 15 s for the inverter's reading to catch up), the load goes back to what the production did follow - stopping, if that is under its minimum - and nothing more is tried for its *Charge pause duration* (a plug's or tank's *Minimum off time*) plus one *Update frequency*, 3 min 15 s by default. Each further try that fails in a row - a start or a step up alike - doubles that wait, up to 30 minutes (6 min 30 s, 13 min, 26 min, then every 30 min), and the first try the production follows brings it back to 3 min 15 s. A charger's *Charging Status* says so while it waits - for example *Insufficient Solar (no spare sun on 3 tries, next try 14:32)* - and carries `sun_probe_failed_tries` and `sun_probe_next_try_at`, as does every load's *Available Current* sensor. In closed-loop tests with 4 kW of sun and a 1 kW house a car started on the first command and reached the 13.0 A the sun leaves in 48 s; where the house takes all the sun, or leaves less than the car's minimum, it ran for 48 s and stopped, and over a 12-hour day it was tried 26 times instead of 179 (once about every 4 minutes); a car already charging at the edge of the sun is offered its next step just as seldom. The price is that sun which comes back is found up to 30 minutes late (it was up to about 4). A try is capped like any offer, never past the inverter's rating or a phase's leg; what it may ask beyond the sun is one step, for up to 62 s on a start and 83 s on a growth step (which comes back down through the charger's ramp). An inverter that trips on overload may still drop out on each failed try: a longer *Charge pause duration* makes every wait longer, and a solar production sensor beside the output sensors measures the spare sun, so nothing needs trying. This replaces the note above that a charger which is not already charging no longer starts. Sites with a battery, with a solar sensor beside output sensors, and grid-tied sites are unchanged.
- **A battery whose power reading is lost no longer has its discharge offered twice**: when the battery power sensor is unavailable for longer than a minute (or none is configured) Load Juggler cannot see what the battery is doing, and beside a solar production sensor it assumed it was idle and offered the chargers its whole discharge rating. A self-consumption battery is rarely idle - it covers the house and then the car - and the discharge carrying the car already shows up as spare once the car's own draw is set aside, so it was offered twice: at night a car the battery was carrying rose to **32 A**, the battery stopped at its rating and the grid carried **3.4 kW** past a 0 W import allowance (1.4 kW past 2 kW) until the reading came back. Without the reading the battery's spare discharge is now offered only as far as the meter shows it: a car the battery is already carrying keeps its charge, with an import allowance a car still climbs to what the site really has (26.1 A at 2 kW), and with no allowance a car that is not yet charging waits for the reading to return. Sites without a solar production sensor already worked this way. Off-grid sites are unchanged.

- **A charger whose current reading gets stuck no longer makes charging stop and start**: some chargers go on obeying their charging profiles while the current or power they report stops changing - most often pinned at 0 while the car charges. Load Juggler works out what the house itself is drawing by taking every managed load's reading off the meter (off-grid: off the inverter's output), so every amp a charger stuck at 0 does not report was booked as house load. With a 6 kW allowance and a 1 kW house, a charger started at 5 kW made the "house" jump to 6 kW, the allowance was gone, the charger was cut and paused, the "house load" vanished with it, and after the pause it all began again - reproduced in a closed-loop test as a stop every pause for as long as the reading stayed stuck. A reading stuck too high did the opposite damage: current the car had stopped taking was handed out again as headroom. Load Juggler now notices a stuck reading from evidence it already has, and neither test is a timeout. **The reading claims more than the charger may now deliver** - it was cut to 8 A and still reads 16, or the charger itself says no energy is flowing - for longer than twice the longest gap that reading normally goes between updates, which is measured on each charger rather than configured. **Or the house load moves with Load Juggler's own commands to that charger** - up when it is started or raised, down when it is cut - twice in a row and both ways, on the charger's own phases, while its reading does not move at all. A car charging steadily, taking less than it is offered, or taking nothing contradicts neither, and a house appliance that happens to switch on as the charger starts is one coincidence, not two. While a reading is judged stuck the charger is controlled **on the current it was told to draw**, and it is reserved in full so the other loads stay inside your limits; in the same test the charger stopped once and then held the 6 kW allowance to the end. It is plain to see: the charger's *Charging Status* reads, for example, "Charging (readout stuck - controlled on assumed current)", one warning is logged when it starts, and *Current Managed Power*, *Household Power* and the charger's *Allocated Current* show the estimated figures with an `estimated: true` attribute (with the evidence and since when) instead of a measurement. The charger's *Available Current* sensor carries the details as `readout_*` attributes - the frozen value, the assumed current, the reading's normal update gap. It ends by itself once the reading moves again - twice, so a single update after a reconnect does not count - or when the car is unplugged. Off-grid sites are covered the same way, against the inverter's output.
- **A charger ramping up is no longer permitted past its allowance**: the site's spare capacity is worked out by taking the managed loads' draw off the meter reading, and both figures are smoothed so they move together - a smoothed meter minus an unsmoothed draw is what made a modulating load hunt around its target. Since 2.1.1 the draw's smoothing was stepped twice every update instead of once, so while a charger ramped up its draw ran ahead of the meter, the household read low for a few updates, and the charger was permitted the difference on top of its allowance. In a closed-loop test on a breaker-limited phase (25 A breaker, 8 A household, so 17 A for the car) a car ramping at 1 A/s was permitted up to **414 W** past the allowance for about half a minute and the site drew up to **345 W** over its breaker; a car that jumps straight to full current, **690 W** and **414 W**. Every figure built on that subtraction leaned the same way while a load ramped, the Excess surplus and the battery charge-limit advice included, and the two disagreed with each other about what the loads were drawing. The draw is now smoothed once per update and every figure reads that one value; through the same ramp the permit holds exactly at the allowance. The smoothing itself, its time constant and what it filters, is unchanged.
- **A car starting to charge is no longer permitted past its allowance**: once a charger's measured draw has held steady for the *Draw settle time* while clearly below what it was offered, Load Juggler treats it as what the car really takes and hands the rest of its share to the other loads. A steady 0 A counted too when it was steady only because no energy could flow yet - an empty connector, a car just plugged in and waiting in *Preparing*, or one paused in *SuspendedEV* - so when charging began while the meter still read 0, the charger was booked at 0 A instead of its minimum and permitted that minimum on top of everything the site had spare. In a closed-loop test on a breaker-limited phase (25 A breaker, 8 A household, so 17 A for the car) a car plugged in was permitted **23 A** - 1380 W over the allowance - from its first moment in *Preparing* until it had been charging for one update, and a car waiting in *Preparing* or paused in *SuspendedEV* was permitted it from 15 s into the wait onwards; within a 30 s wait the command sent to the charger climbed to 22.2 A, and two chargers starting together took the site to 26 A through its 25 A breaker. The settle time now runs only while the connector reports *Charging*, and starts again each time charging does; through the same starts the permit holds at 17 A. A charging car that takes less than it is offered still hands the difference to the other loads as before. One side effect: a car paused in *SuspendedEV* now keeps its share reserved until the 60 s pause grace ends, rather than handing it on after 15 s.
- **An off-grid charger that starts is no longer permitted past the inverter's rating**: with no grid meter, Load Juggler works out what the house is drawing as the inverter's output minus the managed loads' draw, and sizes the chargers on whatever of the inverter's rating that leaves. The output is smoothed, but the draw taken off it was not, so when a charger started its whole draw came off at once while the output it is part of was still catching up - for a few updates the house seemed to shrink, and the charger was handed the difference. In a closed-loop test on a 6 kW inverter carrying a 1 kW house (so 5 kW for the car), a car that jumps straight to full current was permitted up to **773 W** past the allowance and the inverter ran up to **566 W** over its rating; a car ramping at 1 A/s, **980 W** and **911 W**. With the full charger control loop every start ran the inverter **520 W** over for about half a minute, and **about 200 W** over for a minute after a stuck reading was detected. The house figure now takes off the same once-per-update smoothed draw as every other figure (the fix above), and through the same starts the permit holds at the allowance. The smoothing itself is unchanged: a 2 kW spike in a single output reading still moves the permit by 600 W, not 2 kW. Two things follow from taking the one draw: a hybrid inverter on a grid-tied site gets the same correction to its per-phase house figure, and off-grid a load with Dynamic Control off now counts as house load, as it already did grid-tied - its draw used to be taken off the house figure without being reserved either, so the inverter's allowance was worked out as if it were free.
- **Off-grid with a solar sensor and no inverter output sensors, a charger no longer counts its own draw as house load**: on such a site the one measure of what the site draws is solar production plus battery discharge - the inverter's whole supply, the chargers included. Load Juggler took that figure as the house load without taking the managed loads' own draw off it, so a charger's draw was counted against itself: every amp it took shrank what the inverter had left for it by as much. In a closed-loop test on a 6 kW inverter carrying a 1 kW house (so 21.7 A for the car), the car settled at **10.9 A** - it met its own draw halfway. The managed draw now comes off that figure too, the same once-per-update smoothed draw every other figure takes off, and in the same test the car settles at **21.7 A** with the inverter never past its rating. Grid-tied sites were not affected: there the draw was already taken off through the grid meter.
- **Off-grid, a charger limited by the battery no longer counts its own draw as used**: off-grid the battery's discharge carries every managed load's draw, and the capacity offered to the loads was solar plus the battery's discharge rating *less the discharge in flight* - so whatever a charger was already drawing was booked as spent. Grid-tied the managed draws are handed back when they are taken off the meter reading; off-grid there is no reading to take them off, and nothing handed them back. With a 1 kW house on a 5 kW battery, a car drawing 3 kW was offered the 1 kW left - under its 6 A minimum - and cut; the discharge fell back to the house, the 4 kW returned, and it started again: in the scenario test it swung between 17.4 A and 0 on every update, and in a closed-loop test it was cut after every start, paused and restarted for as long as it ran. A Solar Only car at its battery target hunted the same way on the surplus it was using. The same term went wrong the other way on a site with a solar production sensor and no inverter output sensor, which added the battery's full rating and never took the house off: a car was offered 28.3 A and 7.6 kW was asked of the 5 kW battery. The loads are now offered the site's supply less the house - solar plus the battery's rating, minus the household - worked out as the supply they already hold plus the rating less the battery's flow, on the same smoothed draw the grid-tied path uses so a starting car cannot run ahead of the battery reading: the car above holds 17.4 A and the battery 5.0 kW from the first command. Below the battery's minimum SOC the loads get the solar surplus and nothing from the pack (before: 1 kW of it, on a 3 kW array with a 1 kW house). And on a solar-sensor site the managed loads are no longer counted as household, which is what made an inverter-limited car hunt there.
- **A car taking less than its minimum current is no longer permitted past the site's limit**: once a charging car's draw has held steady below what it was offered, Load Juggler books only that draw against the site's capacity, so the current it is not using is free for other loads. A car can hold below its *minimum* that way - paused at 0 A while still Charging, or tapering near full - and its permit was then filled as if the whole minimum had been taken: the minimum was counted once as reserved and again as still free, so the permit came out at the minimum plus everything the site had left. In a closed-loop test on a breaker-limited phase (25 A breaker, 8 A household, so 17 A for the car) a car paused at 0 A was permitted **23 A** in Priority mode and its full **32 A** in Shared, whose equal-split rounds handed the same untouched capacity out again every round; its command climbed toward that while it sat idle, and when it resumed the site drew up to **0.7 A** over its breaker (Shared **1.1 A**; a car that jumps straight to full current, **6 A** and **15 A**). A car tapering to 3 A was permitted 20 A (Shared 32 A). The same double count reached the solar surplus - a Solar Only car at 0 A on 10 A of surplus was permitted 16 A (Shared 32 A), the difference drawn from the grid - and a power station drawing nothing, which is booked at its measured draw every update. A load's permit is now what the site has left plus what the load itself is booked at: 17 A for the paused car in every mode and no overshoot when it resumes, the capacity it does not use still goes to the loads behind it, and a car at 0 A is still offered at least its minimum so it can start again on its own. Sequential - Strict and Sequential - Optimized size each load once against what is left and were never affected. Two visible changes follow: in Shared mode a car self-limited *above* its minimum is permitted its equal share instead of being fed on to its maximum (a car capped at 16 A beside a second car on a 50 A site: 25 A, was 32 A), and off-grid a charger whose reading is stuck at 0 now takes the same one pause before it is controlled on its command that it takes on a grid-tied site.
- **Off-grid with inverter output sensors and *Parallel* wiring, a charger no longer counts its own draw as house load**: with no grid, everything the site uses - the chargers included - comes out of the inverters, whichever wiring is chosen. On *Series* wiring Load Juggler took the managed loads' draw off the inverter's output to find the house; on *Parallel* - the setup form's default - it worked the house out from the grid meter plus the inverter's output, and off-grid the grid meter reads nothing, so the house was the inverter's whole output with the chargers still in it. Every amp a charger took shrank what the inverter had left for it by as much. In a closed-loop test on a 6 kW inverter carrying a 1 kW house (so 21.7 A for the car), the car settled at **10.9 A** - it met its own draw halfway - and the published household figure read **3508 W** for a 1000 W house. Off-grid both wirings now take off the same once-per-update smoothed draw, and in the same test the car settles at **21.7 A** with the inverter never past its rating, the household reads the house, and the two wirings give the same permit on every update. A fleet mixing both wirings already took the draw off once and still does. Grid-tied sites are not affected.
- **Off-grid with no inverter output sensors, a charger is no longer given the house's share of the inverter at night or on a battery-only site**: on such a site Load Juggler works out what the house draws as solar production plus battery discharge, less the managed loads' own draw - but it did so only while a solar production sensor read more than 0 W. With the solar sensor reading 0 W at night, or with no solar sensor at all, the house counted as drawing nothing and the inverter's whole rating went to the chargers. In a closed-loop test on a 6 kW inverter carrying a 1 kW house the car was given **26.1 A** instead of 21.7 A, and the inverter ran **1003 W** over its rating - exactly the house. With a solar sensor the site changed its mind at dusk: about a minute after the panels reached 0 W the permit stepped from 21.7 A to 26.1 A, for the rest of the night. A solar reading of 0 W now counts like any other, and with no solar sensor the battery's discharge is the supply: in the same test the car gets 21.7 A day and night, dusk moves nothing, and the inverter stays within its rating. Where nothing measures what the inverter is carrying - a battery whose power sensor is missing, or unavailable for longer than a minute - Load Juggler now hands out none of the inverter's capacity instead of all of it; there the car was given the whole 26.1 A too, by day as well as by night, and the hub's status already names the missing or unavailable sensor. On a battery-only site that does have solar panels, the part of the house the panels cover is still invisible without a production sensor: add a solar production sensor or inverter output sensors. Grid-tied sites are not affected.

## 2.1.1

### New Features

- **Filters page, and every filter behind it now measured in seconds**: a hub's *Configure* menu gains **Filters**, beside *Timing and detection*. It exposes eight numbers that were always there and never adjustable: the smoothing on the site's readings and on each load's permit, how quickly a modulating load closes the gap to its permit, the battery controller's fast filter, how long an EVSE's draw must hold before it counts as settled, the dead band below which a load is not re-commanded, and the minimum rates a permit rises and falls at. Every default is the value the engine has always used, so a hub that never opens the page behaves exactly as before - not approximately, identically. The page refuses one combination: the battery controller's fast filter must be shorter than the site reading filter, or the two stop working as a pair. The minimum ramp-down rate also sets how far a charger's offered current may lag its command before it counts as ignoring it, and the label says so. **Behind the page, a correction**: several of these were counted in *cycles*, which is a duration only once you know how often the site refreshes. A site polled every 60 s to be gentle on its inverter's Modbus was silently getting a ~200 s smoothing filter where the default site had ~6 s, and a charger polled every 60 s waited about five minutes before a profile it was ignoring was re-sent, where one polled every 15 s waited one. They are all seconds now - the reading and permit filters, the ramp response, the draw-settle time and the profile re-send - so changing a refresh rate no longer retunes the control loop. **Sites at the default 2 s refresh are unaffected**: every time constant was chosen to reproduce the previous behaviour there exactly. Sites polling slower track a moving surplus noticeably better; on the test rig, mean tracking error at a 10 s refresh had been **1,164 W** against **596 W** at 1 s with nothing but the refresh rate changed, and the two now land within run-to-run noise of each other.
- **Inverter features page**: an inverter's setup now opens with what the inverter *has* - **PV array connected**, **Battery connected**, **Battery charge management** - and only the pages for the ticked features follow. A PV-only string inverter never sees the battery or write-control pages, and stores nothing for them; the options flow opens on the same page, and unticking a feature clears its settings on save. Existing inverter entries are migrated once: the features are inferred from what each entry had configured (a production sensor or forecast device → PV array; an SOC or power entity → battery; a charge register or SOC slots → charge management) and the leftover defaults of everything else are cleared - which is what removes the phantom battery of the bug fix below.
- **Forecast accuracy and clipped energy now exclude only real curtailment**: both observers skipped every interval where Excess mode was engaged. On a site whose charge control deliberately parks export one trigger margin under the limit, that is most of the productive afternoon - yet nothing is being thrown away there, because the battery is absorbing the surplus. The result was a *Forecast accuracy* sensor stuck on Unknown for days and a gain that never moved off 1.00. Both now gate on physical curtailment instead: the meter actually sitting on the export wall.
- **The stored forecast-gain series really does survive a restart now**: the hub's first calculation cycle creates the gain observer before the *Forecast accuracy* sensor is added, and the restore refused to touch an observer that already existed - so the saved fortnight was discarded on every restart and the series never grew past the current session (three days of uptime, three blocks). The restore now merges: the stored series is adopted, whatever the running session has already measured is kept, and an observer that genuinely has its own history is left alone.
- **The forecast gain is learned from a stored 15-minute series and survives restarts**: each array's *Forecast accuracy* sensor now keeps the last 14 days of forecast-vs-measured energy in 15-minute blocks (curtailed blocks excluded, as before) and carries it across Home Assistant restarts in its restore data - nothing is written to the recorder. The learned gain is recomputed from that series on every block, as one energy-weighted ratio, and published as the sensor's `gain` attribute together with `gain_hourly`: per hour-of-day multipliers on the overall gain (not independent hourly gains), so the overall figure still says how far the forecast is off and the offsets say where in the day. `gain_days` and `gain_blocks` show how much history backs it. Today's accuracy stays a same-day figure and is not persisted, except across a restart within the same day.
- **Off-grid sites now get the PV clipping forecast**: the forecast was gated on a grid export limit, and off-grid there is none - so the site where the question matters most had the whole feature silently switched off. A limit of 0 means two opposite things: grid-tied it means the grid absorbs everything and nothing can clip; off-grid it means nothing can leave at all, so every watt above the house must be stored or curtailed. Off-grid hubs now publish clippable, storable and battery-headroom-deficit energy (measured against base consumption alone), and the forecast accuracy observer runs there too, judging curtailment by the battery being full or at its charge rate instead of by a meter that does not exist. The SOC ceiling and charge-rate advice stay suppressed off-grid on purpose: throttling the battery there curtails the surplus on the spot instead of exporting it, and holding SOC down to protect the afternoon only curtails the morning instead.
- **Off-grid sites no longer report their own loads as export**: the reconstructed "export with managed loads off" figure added the managed draws to the grid readings, which off-grid are synthetic zeros - so a site with no grid connection published its loads' consumption (3.1 kW on one) as export. It is now not published at all off-grid, and the Overview omits both grid lines there rather than stating a confident 0 W about a connection that does not exist.
- **The live Overview shows the forecast**: the hub's Overview gains a PV forecast section - the next clipping window's clippable energy, what the battery can still store and what has nowhere to go, the advised battery ceiling, the fleet charge limit with its gate state, clipped energy so far today and yesterday, and the forecast accuracy and peakiness observers. An inverter's Overview shows its charge limit's gate state and, once the per-array observers have data, its own accuracy and learned gain.
- **Download diagnostics**: every Load Juggler device now has Home Assistant's *Download diagnostics* button, and it dumps the whole site in one JSON file - every entry's settings (hub, loads, groups, inverters), the last published site result, and the carried state including each array's stored forecast-gain series. Pressing it on a load gives the same site-wide file, since a load's own settings rarely explain what it was allocated. Nothing is redacted: the integration stores entity ids, priorities and setpoints, no credentials - though entity ids do carry your own device names, worth knowing before posting one publicly.
- **Settings that only seed a slider now say so**: the boiler temperatures, the power station's charge powers and reserves, a plug's power rating and an EVSE's min/max current all exist twice - once in the options as a starting value, once as a slider on the device that owns it afterwards. Editing the option after setup changed nothing, silently (one site's options read 30/80 °C while its sliders held 20/75). Each of those fields now says which one wins.
- **Options menus, one page per question**: an inverter's *Configure* now opens on a menu - Features, Inverter, and then PV, Battery and Battery charge management only for the features it declares - and a hub's opens on Grid connection, Export limit and Excess, Battery and forecast policy (only while an inverter on it has a battery), Timing and detection, and Device priority order (only while it has loads). Every page saves on its own, so changing one setting no longer round-trips seventeen others through a single form. The setup wizards are unchanged.
- **SOC limit meaning (charge ceiling vs discharge floor), and a floor-aware SOC control**: a new per-inverter setting next to the SOC ceiling entities, declaring what *writing* to those entities means on your hardware - and the SOC write-control now honours it. On a genuine ceiling register ("stop charging at N%") nothing changes: the default (**Charge ceiling**) keeps the slots tracking the lower of your source value and the recommendation, exactly as before. On a Deye the slot SOC is a **discharge floor plus grid-charge target** - the same number tells the inverter "grid-charge up to N% and never discharge below it" - so holding the slots at the destination all night blocked overnight self-consumption and actively imported toward it. Set **Discharge floor** and the control writes only in each direction's safe sense: the night before a forecast clip the slots are **lowered to the reserve** (the house drains the battery to it, so the morning has real absorption room), and once the reservation releases they are **raised back to your source value** - an owner-raised slot (say a storm hold at 100%) is never lowered outside a reservation, and nothing is ever raised above the source. The release needs no stored state, so a Home Assistant restart mid-window changes nothing. Two prerequisites are enforced for floor writes, with a log warning when unmet: the *Normal SOC ceiling source* must be set (the 100% fallback written into a floor register would mean *never discharge*), and it must be a **separate helper** - an `input_number` holding your everyday value - not one of the managed slots themselves, since writing the reserve into the entity the destination is read from would drag both down. The setting still does **not** change what is *read*: the charge destination always comes from the source when it is set; a floor register whose value is *not* your target (say a 20% cheap-tariff floor) simply leaves the source unset and the destination anchors at 100% (SOC writes then stay off on such a site).
- **Written charge limits are snapped to the register's own step**: many inverters hold this register in whole units - Deye's *Battery Max Charge Current* is integer amps, so written 6.2 it holds 6. The write decision compares against the register's live value, so that silently-dropped fraction was a standing disagreement between what Load Juggler wanted and what it read back. Harmless while the deadband is wider than the device's resolution, and a write on **every cycle for ever** the moment it is not - the exact Modbus and EEPROM churn the deadband exists to prevent, caused by the deadband being narrower than the register's quantum. Load Juggler now reads the number entity's advertised `step` and snaps every value it writes to that grid, so the read-back matches what was asked for. Rounding is **down**, because every value written here is a *permit* - a ceiling on what the battery may draw - and rounding up would hand out more than the advice allowed, which on the reservation path is headroom the forecast was trying to keep. A register that advertises no step behaves exactly as before.
- **The charge control now follows export peaks faster than it lets them fade**: the battery charge advice reads export and battery power through its own smoothing, one that moves quickly *toward* a limit (deeper export, heavier import) and slowly back toward zero - two cycles to follow a rise, instead of the long average everything else on the site reads. On a real afternoon that is the difference between a short cloud-lensing peak being absorbed into the battery and being curtailed at the export wall: on the cloudy-household-day rig curtailment halves (45 → 22 Wh) with *fewer* register writes (132 → 113), and the burst-train and passing-cloud rows fall by half as well. Two deliberate limits: it is not instant - a single reading is exactly what a motor's start-up inrush looks like, and following readings in full made the Excess verdict flap through the charge allowance on the rigs - and it is private to the charge control: the Excess verdict and the load allocation keep the symmetric smoothing they always had, so nothing else on the site changes behaviour. The battery-power side is smoothed in mirror (fast when charging falls), which keeps the two terms of the advice consistent through a permit cut instead of reading the same transition twice. No setting - the weight is calibrated against the write budgets and fixed.
- **The charge-register write deadband is now absolute watts, and directional**: it used to be a percentage of the register's **normal** value, which is the right scale for a *reduction* - that is where Modbus and EEPROM churn costs something, and the persistence window already gates those. It was the wrong scale for a *rise*. While the charge limit is engaged the advice lives between zero and one *Excess trigger margin* - it is the export overshoot above the setpoint, which is all there is to correct - so a deadband sized to the full register span swallowed roughly half the range the controller actually works in. Observed live: with the battery parked at its destination, an advice of 245–378 W (4.0–6.2 A) against a 4.2 A deadband left the register at 0 A for fifty minutes while export sat ~470 W above its setpoint instead of settling on it. Rises now use a band scaled to the range rises cover - a third of the largest single rise allowed, and never more than your configured deadband, so this can only make the control more responsive upward, never less. Reductions are unchanged. The divisor is calibrated against the closed-loop write budgets rather than picked: a smaller one cost 70% more register writes than the burst-train and cloudy-day tests allow. A side effect worth knowing: the release ramp now lands within a *rise* band of full rate instead of within a full 5% of it, so the register no longer rests a few percent under your normal charge limit after every release. **The setting itself changed units**: *Write deadband* is now **watts** (default **100 W**) instead of a percentage. Watts rather than the register's own unit, so it means the same thing whether your register counts DC amps or watts - which is what the percentage was there for, and converting is the better answer to it. *Upgrading:* a percentage cannot be converted to watts on your behalf (the normal value is read from an entity, not stored), and reading an old `5` as 5 W would be no deadband at all, so the old setting is **dropped on upgrade** and the 100 W default applies. If you had tuned it, set it again. Note the two directions coincide at the default - 100 W is already below a third of a 500 W trigger margin - so the directional half only matters if you raise the deadband well above that. The trade is measured: on the closed-loop rigs the tighter default costs ~10–40% more register writes (132 against 120 over a cloudy day, 14 against 10 through a two-hour burst train) and the curtailment figures are unchanged. If your inverter's firmware is one that writes this register to EEPROM and you would rather have the old thriftiness, raise it - 400 W reproduces roughly the previous behaviour on a 187 A register.
- **Clipped Energy Today**: a hub sensor tracking what the site *actually* threw away, in kWh, resetting at local midnight - the measured counterpart to the advisory **Forecast Clippable Energy**, and the two side by side are what tells you whether the battery reserve is doing its job. Curtailed energy cannot be metered, because the inverter never produces it, so this is the forecast's excess over measured production, accumulated only while the site is **saturated**: the export allowance used up *and* the battery taking all it can. That is exactly the Excess trigger's own verdict, reused rather than re-tested, so the two can never disagree. Being forecast-based it is an **estimate** bounded by the forecast's own accuracy, and it errs in the same direction - a forecast reading 10% high inflates it. The new *Forecast Accuracy* sensor is what measures that bias, on the site's unconstrained intervals, so the two are meant to be read together. Attributes carry how many hours the site spent saturated and yesterday's total.
- **Forecast accuracy and peakiness observers (measure only)**: two new sensors that watch how well the PV forecast is actually doing, and change nothing at all. **Forecast accuracy** (one per inverter, `%`) is today's measured production divided by that inverter's own forecast, energy-weighted - 100% means the forecast is exactly right for that array, 90% that it reads 10% high. Intervals where the site was *curtailing* are excluded, because while an inverter is throttling its array the measured production is suppressed by the very thing being forecast; that exclusion is per interval rather than per day, so a clipping day still contributes its honest morning and evening (dropping such days whole would leave the measurement learning only from overcast ones). A running per-array average and the number of contributing days ride along as attributes. **Forecast peakiness** (one per hub, `%`) is how much the site actually clipped against what a 15-minute average predicted it would: 100% means the block average told the whole truth, and anything above is the gap that averaging hides - clipping is one-sided, so a block average can never *over*state it. It is measured from the site's own production samples, with no cloud model and no clear-sky estimate involved. Both exist to answer, with a season of real data, whether a *learned* forecast correction is worth applying - nothing is applied today, and the reserve is computed exactly as before. Note the accuracy sensor needs the forecast configured per inverter (the hub-level legacy forecast field has no array to attribute a measurement to), and both reset on a restart: each day's figure is published as the sensor's own state, so the history is kept by the recorder regardless.

### Improvements

- **Excess loads start in priority order**: the Excess verdict used to start every Excess load at once - a modulating load at its minimum, a binary one at its rating - whatever the surplus, so a 2.1 kW boiler and an EcoFlow could both engage on a 300 W surplus, overrun it, release, and repeat every few minutes (with a backup-reserve write on every flip). Now only the highest-priority Excess consumer starts on the verdict alone; each one behind it starts, and keeps running, only while surplus is still left after the higher-priority loads' permits. The leftover need not cover the load's own minimum (400 W left after the boiler still starts a 1.4 kW-minimum EVSE at its floor), but nothing left means it waits its turn. A grid-backed load that has settled below its permit claims only what it actually draws, so it never blocks surplus it is not using.
- **Multi-battery sites: the charge-limit advice is now divided by remaining headroom**: with two or more battery inverters on one hub, the fleet's recommended charge limit used to be split in proportion to each inverter's *Battery max charge power*, so the pack behind the bigger charger filled first and sat at the ceiling while the other was still climbing. It is now divided by the room each pack still has under the advised SOC ceiling (capacity × percent to go), clamped at each inverter's own charge cap with the excess offered to the packs that still have room - the packs reach the ceiling together, which is also how the SOC advice already shares the reserve. Single-battery sites are unaffected.

### Bug Fixes

- **Excess loads no longer over-commit the surplus**: on a real site an Excess margin of **74 W** started **4,100 W** of load, and the loads then cycled on and off together every few minutes under a rock-steady sky. Solar production did not rise when they started, so nothing was recovered from curtailment - the power came straight off what would have been exported. Four things were wrong, all now fixed. **The trigger's release band was counted as surplus**: the deadband that holds an engaged load through a momentary dip was added to the pool that *sizes* loads, so a site 489 W over its trigger published 989 W of available surplus for as long as anything stayed on. The band still holds the verdict, it no longer inflates the pool. **A boiler boosting on surplus ignored the surplus entirely**: Freeze Protection and Normal are full-power modes, so once the verdict pushed the setpoint to boost the element took its whole rating from the site's physical capacity however little was spare - while still claiming against the Excess order and squeezing out lower-priority loads. A boosting boiler is now gated on the surplus existing, and it remains all-or-nothing at its full rating (overshooting costs exported energy, never imported). Crucially it is **never** gated below the temperature its own mode asks for, so frost protection and the everyday setpoint still run unconditionally - only the free-energy part above them is refusable. **An importing phase now cancels an exporting one**: on a three-phase site with unbalanced household the surplus was summed per phase with imports clamped away, so a site exporting 10 A on two phases while pulling 10 A in on the third read as 20 A of surplus when its true position was 10 A. Both the Excess verdict and the pool now net the phases. That figure stays *gross* where it belongs - the export limit itself, and the charge-limit advice that steers on it - because a contractual export limit counts exported flow per phase. **And a claim on one phase now counts against loads on the others**, so a boiler claiming 2 kW on one leg no longer leaves a station on another leg reading the same site-wide headroom as untouched. A note for automations: the hub's *Excess margin* sensor is the verdict, so it now reads net too - on an unbalanced site it will show a smaller number than before, and that number is the site's real position.
- **An unplugged charger no longer reports the current it was last drawing**: many OCPP chargers stop sending meter readings the moment a session ends, and Home Assistant keeps a sensor's last value until something newer arrives - so the charger's current reading froze at whatever the car had been taking and stayed there for hours. Load Juggler believed it. Because the site's spare capacity is worked out by subtracting managed loads from the meter, one stale reading moved four published figures at once: on a site exporting 856 W with a 1.8 A import on one phase, *Grid headroom* showed the entire main breaker as free, a solar surplus of 11.4 A appeared on a phase that had none, *Export with managed loads off* read 3,898 W, and *Household* clamped to 0 W. A load engaging on that phantom surplus would have made the site import. An empty connector now reports no draw, since a connector with no car in it cannot be drawing current - which is the same fact the charger's own status already reports as "Unplugged". Deliberately narrow: only a connector positively reporting **Available** (OCPP's own word for "no car") is zeroed. A status that cannot be read is *not* evidence of an empty connector and keeps its reading, as do *Finishing* and *Faulted*, where a car may still be plugged in. It is not a staleness timeout either - a car charging steadily holds its reading unchanged for minutes, so "has not updated recently" cannot tell a stale reading from a steady one.
- **New sensor: Household Power**: what the site draws that Load Juggler does not control - everything the meter sees, less every managed load. The figure was always computed and shown on the hub's Overview page; it is now an entity of its own, so it can be graphed in the energy dashboard and used in automations. Nothing about the engine changes - it is the same number, exposed.
- **"Set charging profile failed" after a car finishes charging**: on a charger left plugged in after a completed session, Load Juggler kept sending charging profiles to a session it had already decided was over - one per update interval, for as long as the car stayed connected. Chargers that refuse those profiles raise a warning notification for each attempt, so the symptom was *Ocpp integration - Warning: Set charging profile failed with response Exception*, always with a car plugged in, always starting when that car finished, and sometimes several within an hour. Reported on an EvBox Elvi and present since at least 1.1. Nothing was mis-controlled - the car was finished and the profiles carried 0 A - but the notifications are alarming and they buried real ones. **The cause** was two layers disagreeing about one connector. A finished car does not report "Finishing"; it sits in **SuspendedEV** - plugged in, drawing nothing, session still open - which is also what a car briefly pausing for battery balancing reports, so the engine waits a minute of no draw before it calls the session over. It did that correctly, and internally rewrote the connector's state to do it. The part that sends profiles never saw that rewrite: it re-read the charger's own reported state, saw "SuspendedEV", and carried on. It now uses the same verdict the rest of the engine reached, so the writing stops when the deciding stops. The one-minute grace is unchanged, so a car that pauses mid-session and resumes is unaffected.
- **A single-phase Excess load is no longer capped at a third of the site's surplus**: the Excess pool charged every phase an even share of the export allowance, so a load could reach only its own third of the surplus however much its phase was actually exporting. Measured on the test rig: a 2,400 W power station on phase C was held to 1,564 W while phase C exported 4,965 W and the site had 4,394 W of surplus - and because the inverter holds the export limit by throttling the array, the watts it was not allowed to absorb were not exported either, they were simply never produced. The permit flat-topped a third of the way up and the rest of the swing showed up as curtailment. A third of the limit is not a bound that exists: an export limit is contractually a site **total**, and a 3x25 A connection exporting 15/20/25 A is compliant rather than pegged at 20/20/20. Each phase is now bounded by its own export flow and the site by its total, which are both real quantities - and taking a phase's whole value brings it to exactly zero export, never into import, so this cannot make a phase start buying. **What you will see:** on an unbalanced site, or any site whose Excess loads sit on one or two phases, those loads now draw more than before and export sits closer to the limit. That is the surplus they were always meant to absorb. Three-phase loads and balanced sites are unaffected, since there the even third and the real per-phase flow were already the same number.
- **A charge limit of zero is no longer swallowed by the write deadband**: with the forecast advising 0 W, a register left holding a token 1 A stayed there indefinitely - the remaining gap (~51 W) is smaller than any useful deadband, so the write never fired and the battery kept taking a trickle. Zero now always writes. A configured *minimum charge limit* is unaffected: it never asks for 0 in the first place.
- **A sensor dropout no longer releases the battery's destination hold**: the "battery is at its destination" latch now holds its last answer while the SOC reads unknown, instead of resetting. A 90 s Modbus dropout used to take the charge advice from a few hundred watts straight to the full rate until the reading returned.
- **Excess loads about to start count in the start order before they draw**: a boiler in Freeze Protection or Normal mode below its boost temperature now claims its rating in the Excess start order from the cycle the verdict turns on, before its thermostat has responded, so a lower-priority station or plug no longer wins the first cycle and gets cut a cycle later (with a needless reserve write on the way). Plugs in Excess mode claim their rating and modulating Excess loads their minimum the same way.
- **A full power station no longer credits the site with its commanded charge speed**: with no AC input/output sensors configured, a power station's draw falls back to the charge speed Load Juggler last wrote. It now falls back to 0 W once the station's battery has reached the backup reserve it is holding, since the station stops drawing from the wall there whatever speed is set. Before, a station sitting full kept adding up to its full commanded speed to the reconstructed export, which could hold the Excess verdict on and over-state the site's surplus.
- **The pre-2.4 export threshold is pruned from your config**: `excess_export_threshold` was replaced in 2.4 by the export limit and trigger margin, and the migration that derived the new settings from it left the old key sitting in the entry. Nothing read it; it is now removed.
- **A PV-only inverter no longer counts as a second battery**: the inverter form saves *Battery max charge power* and *Battery max discharge power* at their 5000 W defaults even when no battery entities are set, and the fleet sums counted them. On a site with a hybrid next to a battery-less string inverter, the hybrid was advised only its share of the charge limit - a 4500 W pack beside a 5000 W phantom got 47 % - so the charge register sat far below what the export overshoot called for while the inverter curtailed, and the Excess allowance was 5 kW wider than the site's real battery. Battery figures are now read only from inverters that have a battery SOC or power entity.

## 2.1.0

### New Features

- **Hot Water Tank device type**: Manage a hot water tank as a binary heating load, driven through a Home Assistant `climate` entity (e.g. a Generic Thermostat). Three configurable setpoints (Away / Normal / Boost) and three operating modes - Freeze Protection, Normal, and Solar Priority - that pick a setpoint based on solar surplus and battery state. The climate entity keeps doing the temperature regulation; Load Juggler decides when heating is allowed and which target to write. See the [Charge Modes Guide](CHARGE_MODES_GUIDE.md#hot-water-tank-modes).
- **Portable Power Station device type**: Manage a portable power station (EcoFlow Delta and similar, through a local integration such as [ha-ef-ble](https://github.com/rabits/ha-ef-ble)) as a modulating load. The engine sets its AC charging speed the way it sets an EVSE's current - same four modes, Excess by default - and manages its **backup reserve** as the on/off gate, because a charge-speed knob with a 200 W floor cannot express "don't charge": dropped below the station's battery level, the reserve stops the wall draw entirely and the station spends what it stored on its own loads. A **Storm Reserve** switch holds a high reserve filled from any source, overriding the mode. Min/max charge power are configured rather than read from the device, so a station can be held below its hardware maximum, and only the charging component of the wall draw (`AC input - AC output`) counts as the load's draw - anything plugged into the station is ordinary household consumption. See the [Charge Modes Guide](CHARGE_MODES_GUIDE.md#power-station-modes).
- **EVSE phase mask sensor**: 3-phase EV chargers get a new sensor showing which site phases the car is actively drawing on (e.g. `A`, `AB`, `ABC`, or `Idle`).
- **Multiple inverters, per-inverter batteries**: an inverter is now its own config entry linked to the hub (*Add Inverter / Home Battery*), carrying everything physically attached to it - capacity, topology, output sensors, its PV production sensor and solar forecast device(s), and optionally its own battery. The hub is left with the grid connection and site-wide policy on one **Hub Settings** page. The hub aggregates the fleet - capacity-weighted fleet SOC drives the SOC-gated modes, each battery's charge headroom counts only while *that* battery is below *its* full SOC, and mixed AC-coupled + hybrid sites derive solar correctly per inverter. Existing hubs migrate automatically on startup: the legacy hub-level inverter/battery fields move onto a new inverter entry, hub sensor entity IDs are unchanged (now fleet aggregates). Each inverter device carries its own Solar Production and Battery SOC/Power sensors. **Note:** the *Recommended Battery Max SOC* and *Recommended Battery Charge Limit* sensors moved from the hub to the inverter entries (per battery - that is where the future write-control will act); the fleet values remain available in the hub data for automations.
- **PV clipping forecast (export-limited sites)**: Sites with more PV than they may export (e.g. 15 kWp behind a 5 kW export limit) curtail the midday peak if the home battery fills up on morning production that could have been exported. With a **grid export limit**, a **battery capacity** and one or more **Open-Meteo Solar Forecast** devices configured (the integration creates one device per PV array - select each array's device), the hub computes how much of the remaining day's production cannot be exported or consumed and publishes five advisory sensors: clippable and storable energy (kWh), a **recommended battery max SOC** that reserves exactly the storable amount **below where your battery was heading anyway** - the inverter's *Normal SOC ceiling source*, or 100% when none is configured - and self-heals back to that destination as the peak passes. On a battery that normally stops at 95%, a 2 kWh reserve in a 20 kWh pack holds it at 85% and lets it arrive at 95% by absorbing the clip through the peak; anchored at 100% it would reserve that unused top 5% twice and meet the peak with only 5% of room. Several batteries with different ceilings are averaged by capacity, and a brief dropout of a ceiling source keeps the last value it published rather than snapping the reserve away. From the evening onward those figures roll over to the **next** clipping window rather than falling to zero for the night: once today's clip is spent, the clippable, storable and deficit figures - and the reserve they buy - describe **tomorrow's** peak. The search stops one day out, so a clip the day after tomorrow never holds the battery low through the clear day in between; with nothing today and nothing tomorrow, the recommendation simply rests at the destination. The reserve is not *applied* the moment today's clip runs out, though. The recommended ceiling becomes a discharge floor once an inverter's SOC control fans it out, so dropping it at dusk would park the battery at the reserve by early evening and leave the house on the grid until dawn - the pack only has to be down there by the time production starts. So the recommendation holds at your destination all evening and drops **just in time**, using your **Base consumption** as the overnight discharge estimate and the forecast's own first crossing above that draw as the deadline, with a 20% head start (arriving too full costs clipped kilowatt-hours; arriving early costs nothing). A 20 kWh pack heading for 95% with 2 kWh of clip tomorrow and production from 08:30 holds at 95% through the evening and drops to 85% at 00:30. Once dropped it stays dropped for the night, whatever the pack does; if the battery's SOC is unreadable it holds at the destination rather than evicting a battery on a guess, and with no usable base consumption it applies the reserve straight away. The recommended charge limit's *reservation* half deliberately does **not** follow the new window - it protects headroom at risk *now*, so a clip a night away leaves it released and nothing is written to a charge register in the dark; its *destination* half has no window at all, because a battery at the ceiling you sent it to is held there whatever the forecast says (see below), a headroom deficit for when the battery is already too full, and a **recommended charge limit** that holds battery charging to the surplus the site cannot place, only while the headroom is actually at risk. Advisory by default - feed the values to your inverter with an automation, or let the inverter entry apply them itself (see below). See the [README](README.md#pv-clipping-forecast-export-limited-sites).
- **Inverter battery charge control (opt-in writes)**: An inverter entry can now act on the clipping forecast instead of only reporting it. Point *Battery charge limit entity* at the inverter's own maximum-charge-current register (Deye: `Battery Max Charge Current`), say whether it takes DC amps or watts, and a **Battery Charge Control** switch appears on that device. While it is on, the recommended charge limit is written to the register and the normal value is restored the moment the advice releases - so the battery fills slowly through the morning and still has room for the midday peak the grid would otherwise curtail. While the limit is engaged the value is **direct feedback on your own meter**: each cycle the battery is permitted what it is already absorbing plus however far *reconstructed* export is running over the setpoint, and the setpoint is one *Excess trigger margin* below your export limit - the same margin that already puts the Excess trigger below it. That lands export just under the limit rather than on it, in a single step, whatever the house happens to be doing. Reconstructed export is the draws-credited-back figure the Excess trigger itself decides on, so your own loads' charging can never steer the battery's limit. Sitting a margin below the limit is also what makes this work on inverters that hard-enforce the export limit by curtailing their own PV (most hybrids): a controller aiming exactly at the export ceiling can never see an error, because the inverter throttles production to hold the ceiling - the permit would sit at its floor while real kilowatts were thrown away. A margin lower, the error while export is pinned *is* that margin, so the permit climbs out by one margin per cycle until export falls off the limit and the plain feedback tracks the sun. The value carries **no history at all**: there is no time constant to wait out, nothing to freeze during a cloud, and nothing left over after one - and it never consults your *Base consumption*, which stays exact where it belongs, in the clippable- and storable-energy figures. A base set 200 W low no longer costs you anything at all. A battery that is *selling* rather than absorbing simply makes the first term negative and the value floors at zero, since a charge cap cannot force a discharge. It does want your inverter's *Battery power entity*: without one the value falls back to the export error alone, which is conservative - a real surplus is then admitted only as fast as the meter shows it. Because the value now moves with the meter, the register's pacing is where volatility is absorbed, and it is **directional**. A *lower* limit is written only once every reading in the last *How long a reduction must hold* (default 5 minutes) agrees on it, and then only by the least reduction they all agree on - so a boiling kettle, a passing cloud or a car plugging in reaches the register not at all, and a two-hour train of 3 kW household bursts costs a handful of writes and single watt-hours of curtailment. A *higher* limit is written as soon as it clears the *Write deadband* (default 5% of the normal limit), on the cycle it arrives, still bounded to one Excess trigger margin per write - that bound is exactly the step the masked-site escape climbs at, so the escape is never slowed by a single write. The one exception to the window is the cap **engaging**: the destination hold or the reservation taking hold is protection, not a steady-state correction, and it is written immediately. On a realistic cloudy household day the whole arrangement throws away tens of watt-hours where the previous design threw away hundreds, and it pays for that with a few dozen register writes a day. Above your normal SOC ceiling the battery also becomes the *absorber of last resort*: an engaged Excess load (EVSE, plug, tank boost, power station) gets the surplus first and the battery charges only with what that load cannot absorb, watt for watt. Below the ceiling the battery still comes first - that is what the reserve is for. That ceiling is a **standing** one, not something only a forecast clip switches on: on a day forecast to clip nothing at all the battery still stops charging when it reaches your normal SOC ceiling, because the room above it is the buffer for a day that beats the forecast. Parked there it charges only with what the site genuinely cannot export (and an engaged Excess load takes that first), and it goes back to full rate once the house has drawn it a couple of percent below the ceiling. A site with no *Normal SOC ceiling source* configured anywhere is unaffected: its destination is 100%, which a battery only reaches when it is already full. Releasing is still a ramp rather than a step: the register walks back up to the normal value one margin per paced write (about 19 one-minute steps from a 2 A floor to a 187 A full rate) instead of handing the battery its full rate the instant the advice lets go - that step used to spend the very reserve it had been holding, minutes before the peak it was holding it for - while switching the control **off** restores the normal value in one write, because while disarmed Load Juggler does not touch the register at all. An optional **Minimum charge limit** sets a floor under the value written while charging is being restricted: the recommendation can legitimately be 0, and a battery pinned at a hard 0 stops charging and serves the house from its own cells instead, so its SOC drifts down off the ceiling the advice was holding it at. A couple of amps is enough for it to cover the house draw and sit still. In the register's own unit, like the normal limit; 0 (the default) applies the recommendation as-is, and the floor never applies to the restore write. Default off: nothing is ever written to your inverter until you arm the switch. The device's **Battery Charge Control** sensor reports the limit currently applied to the register as a proper measurement in its own unit (amps or watts) - so it graphs and gets long-term statistics - with the control's standing (`off` / `idle` / `limiting`) as a `control_state` attribute beside it. While the limit is engaged, the Excess trigger counts only the charge rate the register actually permits, so an Excess-mode load engages during a clipping window instead of being held off by charge headroom this control is forbidding. *Upgrading:* the setting formerly called **Minimum time between writes** is now **How long a reduction must hold** - the same stored setting, the same 300 s default, but the direction it paces has changed: lowering the charge limit now needs that long of agreement, while raising it is no longer paced by it at all. If you lowered it (say to 60 s) to make the old symmetric pacing more responsive, **put it back to 300** - responsiveness upward is now free, and a shorter window only buys several times the register writes for a few watt-hours.
- **Inverter battery SOC control (opt-in writes, several entities at once)**: The clipping forecast's *recommended battery max SOC* can now be applied as well as reported - and on the inverters where a SOC ceiling actually exists it does not live in one register. On a Deye it is one `number` per **time-of-use slot**, so *Battery SOC ceiling entities* takes a list: select every slot the battery may charge in and one recommendation is fanned out to all of them. Your everyday ceiling stays yours - point *Normal SOC ceiling source* at the entity your own automations already set (an `input_number`, a template sensor) and Load Juggler writes the **lower** of that and the recommendation, so it can only ever hold the battery below what you asked for, and anything you change there reaches the slots on the next cycle. That entity is also what the clipping reserve is measured down from, so it is worth pointing at your real everyday ceiling even if you never arm the write. Without that entity the everyday ceiling is 100%; configured but unavailable means nothing is written that cycle rather than a ceiling being guessed. A separate **Battery SOC Control** switch arms it, default off, independently of the charge-rate control - an inverter may support either, both or neither. Writes are paced by the same interval setting - now labelled *How long a reduction must hold*, since that is what it means for the charge rate - with a fixed 1-point deadband applied per slot so a slot already at the ceiling is spared the Modbus write, and there is no release write at all: the recommendation climbing back to 100% as the peak passes hands the slots back by itself. A **SOC Control** sensor reports the ceiling being enforced, with each slot's read-back beside it as an attribute.


- **Overview and "How it decides" pages**: Every Load Juggler device's **Configure** button now opens a small menu: edit the settings, or open a live **Overview**. The hub's Overview shows the grid per phase, solar & battery, the computed power pools, unmanaged household consumption, and one line per managed load (mode · priority · permitted vs. drawing · status · circuit-group cap); a load, inverter, or circuit group shows its own slice. Hubs additionally get **"How it decides"** - a plain-language summary of the whole configuration in the order the engine evaluates it, with unconfigured features left out. Both pages are read-only, with Refresh/Back buttons - nothing is saved by viewing them.

### Improvements

- **OCPP charger discovery goes through the device registry**: chargers whose entities were renamed, or whose per-phase sensors live on per-connector sub-devices, are now found and set up correctly instead of being silently skipped. The charger wizard's "OCPP device ID" text box is replaced by a device picker filtered to the OCPP integration - picking a device fills in the charge point id and every current/power sensor at once. Existing chargers are unaffected and need no reconfiguration. **Configure** on an EV charger offers the same picker instead of a typed charge point id - pointing a charger at a different OCPP device moves its charge point id and all of its current/power sensors together, so a mis-matched or replaced charger is fixed in one step.
- **Loads are called loads everywhere**: internally and in config-entry storage, anything that isn't an EV charger (smart plugs, hot water tanks, power stations) is no longer stored or named as a "charger". Existing entries migrate automatically on startup; no entity ids, entity names, attributes or service fields change.
- **One way to edit settings**: the ⋮ *Reconfigure* menu is gone - everything is edited through the **Configure** button, which was always the more complete of the two. One edit path also means a setting can no longer be saved by one flow and ignored by the other.
- **The site is calculated exactly once per cycle**: previously every managed load re-ran the full site calculation on its own timer, so smoothing and settle detection effectively sped up with every load you added, and overlapping updates could send duplicate charging commands. One hub-owned cycle now computes the site once per *Site sensor refresh rate* and serves every load in turn; charging commands are dispatched one at a time.
- **Unmanaged household consumption**: the hub now computes the household draw excluding managed loads (from the solar sensor when present, else derived) and publishes it in the hub data and on the Overview page.
- **Slovenian translation overhaul**: mistranslations and English-style capitalization fixed throughout, terminology unified (loads are *porabniki*, a circuit group is a *deljena varovalka*), and all previously untranslated error messages now have proper Slovenian and English texts.
- **One export number**: the *Excess export threshold* and the forecast's *grid export limit* collapsed into a single **Grid export limit** - the site's physical/contract ceiling. The Excess trigger derives from it as limit − **Excess trigger margin** (new field, default 500 W; inverters curtail just under the limit, so a trigger exactly at it would never fire), and the PV clipping forecast integrates above it. 0 = no export limit: the grid absorbs everything, so Excess never triggers on export and the forecast is off. Existing configs migrate automatically - the limit is seeded as your old threshold + 500 W, keeping the effective trigger point exactly where it was.
- **Excess mode counts the battery as a place to put power**: Excess used to look only at grid export, so a load could start while the home battery still had charge headroom - stealing solar the battery wanted. The trigger is now a single comparison of what the site is *absorbing* (grid export + battery charge power) against what it is *allowed* to absorb (the export allowance + Battery Max Charge Power). Each side drops out when its sink can't absorb: no grid means no export allowance, and no battery - or one at/above its **Battery Full SOC** - means no charge allowance. The allowance is the rate the battery is *permitted* to take, not its plate rating: while an inverter's **Battery Charge Control** is holding its charge register below Battery Max Charge Power for the PV clipping forecast, the difference is not somewhere the site can put power, so it is not counted. Counting it made a clipping window - the one moment the site has surplus it cannot place - read as a site with room to spare, locking Excess loads out for the whole window and dropping an already-engaged one the moment the cap engaged. Advice alone never narrows it: a battery that is only *advised* a lower rate still charges at its plate, and in a multi-inverter fleet only the enforcing inverter's share narrows. **Only *solar* export counts toward the trigger.** The battery's discharge is subtracted from what the meter reads, because `export − battery discharge` is `production − consumption` in every case: a battery serving the house counts *against* the trigger (stored energy keeping the site running is the opposite of surplus), a battery **selling** to the grid is taken away in full, and a charging or idle battery changes nothing at all. Without it, an inverter work mode that sells stored energy (Deye *Selling First*, a time-of-use slot with sell semantics) could push the meter past the trigger on the pack's own energy and start an Excess load on the house battery - charging the car from the battery, at a loss. Sites whose work mode keeps battery discharge off the meter (Zero-Export-to-CT and every equivalent self-consumption mode) see no daylight change; overnight the published margin reads further below the trigger while the pack serves the house - same verdicts, honest arithmetic. `battery_max_charge_power` was previously configured but unused; it now does what its name says. Two consequences worth knowing: Excess can finally trigger **off-grid**, where export is always zero and the battery's charge rate is the only signal, and on an ideal hybrid inverter nothing changes at all, since the battery is only bypassed once it is already saturated.
- **Off-grid Excess reads the battery instead of the meter**: With no grid CTs there is no export to measure, so off-grid the decision rests on the battery - Excess engages once it is charging at its configured maximum. Our own loads' draws are added back into the comparison (grid-tied the feedback loop already does this), which turns a running load into a probe: a curtailing inverter ramps up to serve it and the margin settles at the site's real surplus, instead of the load suppressing the very signal that started it and flip-flopping every cycle. It needs no SOC floor - the battery's measured discharge counts *against* the margin, so a load that outruns production clears the verdict by itself, whatever the combined draw: watts served from the pack are stored energy, not surplus, and can never hold Excess on while the battery drains.
- **One definition of "excess" for every load**: EVSE Excess, smart plug Excess and the hot water tank's boost setpoint now all read the same hub verdict instead of each re-deriving it from export. That verdict also *starts* all of them the same way: a modulating load (EVSE, power station) begins at its **minimum** rate the moment Excess is on - even when the surplus of that moment is smaller than that minimum - and follows the surplus upward from there, the way the on/off loads have always engaged on threshold-hit. A car cannot be charged below the EVSE's minimum at all, so a permit sized to the momentary surplus was a permit the hardware could not express: it mattered most exactly where the surplus is hardest to see, because with **Battery Charge Control** holding export just under the limit the standing surplus sits *at* the trigger and peaks only between register writes - so the charge never started. Releasing is unchanged, and still decided by the **Excess hysteresis** band. The hub publishes the verdict, along with the absorbed and capacity figures, for diagnostics.
- **Excess loads hold on steadily**: an engaged Excess load now releases only when the site genuinely can no longer absorb it - its own draw can no longer switch it off. Previously, on a site importing on one phase while exporting on others, part of an engaged load's draw silently vanished from the Excess comparison and the load could relay-cycle every 10–20 seconds. The comparison now reconstructs the picture as if the load were off (power it freed is credited back to the battery up to the battery's real charging headroom), while export keeps its physical per-phase meaning - importing on one phase never buys export headroom on another, so a configured export limit means what the meter measures. A new **Excess hysteresis** setting (default 500 W) controls how far the surplus may fall below the trigger before an engaged load releases - distinct from the *Excess trigger margin*, which sets where Excess engages below the export limit.
- **Hot water tank boost now triggers on real surplus**: A tank in Freeze Protection or Normal mode raised its target to the Boost setpoint as soon as grid export exceeded the *heating element's own draw* - around 2 kW, which is no measure of site surplus, so the tank boosted on almost any sunny day. Both modes now boost on the hub's Excess verdict - the same one an Excess-mode EVSE or plug triggers on, hysteresis band included - so the tank only boosts on energy the site genuinely cannot absorb anywhere else.
- **Hot water tank yields power while boosting**: A boosting tank now competes at the **Excess** urgency tier instead of its mode's own - heating past the temperature its mode asks for is opportunistic and must not outrank must-run loads. At its Away/Normal floor it keeps its normal tier, and the cold-tank promotion still wins: a Solar Priority tank below its Normal temperature stays at tier 1 even while boosting.
- **Smoother current transitions**: Updated the deadband to a proper Schmitt trigger, and ramps are now applied even if the available current momentarily drops below the minimum the EVSE can offer - a brief consumption spike now just slows the change down instead of stopping it.
- **Excess mode anti-chatter**: Added a hysteresis band to the export threshold so a load in Excess mode no longer flips on/off when export hovers right at the threshold.
- **Battery minimum SOC is now a hard floor**: the SOC hysteresis band around the *Battery Min SOC* setting moved to the safe side. Previously, with a 20% floor and 3% hysteresis, discharge continued down to 17% - the floor you configured was undershot by the hysteresis. Now discharge stops at the configured 20% and resumes once the battery recovers to 23%, so the battery never runs below the floor. The target-SOC band is unchanged (it already sat below the target for the same reason: charging never overshoots the ceiling).
- **Power buffer honored on the breaker limit**: The configured power buffer is now subtracted from the per-phase main-breaker limit as well as the grid-import limit - previously it had no effect on sites without a grid-import limit configured.
- **Off-grid hubs require a battery**: A hub configured without grid CT sensors runs off-grid, where the battery is the primary state signal. Hub setup now requires a battery SOC entity and a battery power entity in that case.
- **Honest, responsive sensors**: the hub, circuit-group, inverter and per-load diagnostic sensors now update the moment the site cycle completes (previously a fixed 10 s poll - snappier on fast sites, less pointless work on slow ones), and report **unavailable** when the calculation hasn't run recently (after a restart before the first cycle, or if the engine stops) instead of freezing at a stale value that looks live. **Site Remaining Power reads *unknown* rather than 0 W when nothing has been calculated** - 0 W is a real, alarming reading ("the site is at its limit") and is no longer used as a placeholder; automations that treated 0 as "no headroom" should also guard for unknown. Numeric sensors now declare proper device classes and units throughout (correct history graphs and unit handling), and Site Remaining Power, Available Current and Allocated Current gain long-term statistics; text sensors (Status, charging status) correctly declare none, ending HA's validation errors about them.
- **Clearer hub sensor names**: The headroom sensors are renamed from "Available …" to "… Remaining Power" (Site Remaining Power, Grid/Solar/Battery Remaining Power, Remaining Current A/B/C) and "Total Managed Power" → "Current Managed Power", to remove ambiguity between power *used*, power *remaining*, and total capacity. Entity IDs are unchanged.
- **Smart plug status sensor**: A smart plug now has a plain "Status" sensor showing `On` / `Off` (plus `Unavailable` / `Not Configured` error states) instead of the EVSE "Charging Status" - the charging-status vocabulary ("Unplugged", "Charging", …) does not apply to a plug.
- **Battery-backed plug solar modes: SOC ladder + inverter coverage**: with a battery configured, a smart plug's modes drain the stored-solar buffer to a progressively higher SOC floor - Solar Priority to the minimum SOC, Solar Only to the target, Excess only near-full - **and** additionally require the inverter to be able to deliver the plug's draw (judged as if the plug were off, so a plug's own draw never sheds itself, and higher-priority loads keep their preemption). Inverter saturation shorter than the *Solar grace period* rides through - on a grid-tied site that window is briefly grid-assisted, the honest price of not flapping the relay on every passing peak; saturation that outlasts it sheds the plug. Set the grace period to how long you are willing to import for (0 = shed immediately). The ride-through never covers the **minimum SOC** itself: that floor is protective, so a plug at it sheds on the cycle it reaches it, and only recovers a full *Battery SOC hysteresis* above it - which is what stops a plug cycling its relay in a one-percent window at the floor, long enough for a car plugged into it to refuse to start charging at all. Bounding how *often* a relay may cycle is a separate setting: **Minimum off time** (plugs and hot water tanks, default 5 minutes) keeps a load off at least that long once it has been switched off, however quickly conditions recover. It never delays switching **off** - only the next switch-on - so it can never hold a load on below a protective floor. Raise it for anything that dislikes being power-cycled: an EV whose outlet is cut mid-negotiation retries, and enough retries in a row lock its onboard charger out until it is unplugged; a compressor needs a rest before restarting. 0 disables it. Same behavior on hybrid grid-tied and off-grid sites (off-grid, the shed protects the inverter itself). Also fixed along the way: the grace window never actually engaged for on/off loads - a permit dip switched a plug or tank off instantly; they now ride through short dips and, if conditions don't recover, shed once instead of duty-cycling the relay. EVSEs are unaffected (they keep modulating).
- **Unified load power - slider plus live measurement**: Smart plugs and hot water tanks each have a power slider (Device Power / Element Power) holding the load's set power, and the hot water tank gets a **new Element Power slider**. When a power-measurement entity is configured, the live measured draw is used directly for the allocated current while the load is on and written back to the slider, so the slider learns and displays the device's real power. Without a measurement entity the slider value is used.

### Bug Fixes

- **A charger configured long ago stops being rejected by the OCPP integration**: Load Juggler versions before February 2026 stored Home Assistant's internal device UUID as the charger's OCPP charge point id. That was fixed for new chargers at the time, but existing ones kept the UUID - and kept working only because the OCPP integration quietly ran any unrecognised id against its first charger, which on a one-charger site was always the right one. **OCPP integration 0.11.2 replaced that fallback with an error**, so every charging profile from such a charger is now refused (`No charger found for device id …`) and the car charges at whatever the charger decides. On startup Load Juggler now checks the stored id against the chargers Home Assistant actually knows about and, when it names none of them, re-identifies the charger from its own Current.Import sensor and repairs the id in place - logging what it changed. Nothing else on the charger is touched. The same check covers the serial numbers older OCPP releases used as a device identifier, and it does nothing at all on a healthy charger. If your charger's sensors were renamed *and* its id is stale, there is nothing left to identify it by: a warning names the charger and re-selecting it on its options page fixes it. Also fixed alongside: the charge-control switch is now found in the entity registry rather than assembled from the charge point id, so a charger whose id contains a capital, a space or a dot - or a multi-connector charger, where the switch carries the connector in its name - is no longer silently left un-enabled.
- **Startup no longer paints a grid spike**: while the grid CT sensors are not yet readable - a fresh restart, or the first moments after saving the hub options - the engine assumes every phase is loaded right up to the main breaker, so a blind site hands out no headroom. It also *published* that assumption as a reading, so Current Grid Power recorded a full-breaker spike (17,250 W on a 3-phase 25 A site) into the history and long-term statistics on every reload. The safety assumption still drives the allocation, but Current Grid Power, exported power and household consumption now report *unknown* until a real reading backs them. A brief CT dropout mid-run is unaffected: the held value is a genuine estimate and keeps publishing as before.
- **A dead solar sensor no longer reports 0 W of production**: a configured solar production sensor that cannot be read - at startup, or after a minute of silence - left *Current Solar Power*, the per-inverter *Solar Production* sensors and *Household Power* showing a confident 0 W, which is right at night and a lie in daylight, and landed in long-term statistics either way. Those sensors now show *unknown* until a real reading arrives; a brief dropout still shows the last known value, and power distribution is unchanged. A healthy inverter in a multi-inverter fleet keeps reporting its own production.
- **A charging car with an unreadable meter no longer reports 0 W of managed power**: when a load's current or power monitor could not be read, *Current Managed Power* and *Household Power* could show 0 W for a load that was drawing kilowatts. They now show *unknown* while any active load's draw is unmeasurable - allocations, permits and *Allocated Current* are unaffected, and an idle load with an offline meter still reports 0 W as before.
- **Sensors clear instead of freezing**: hub and inverter sensors now clear to *unknown* when a figure becomes unavailable mid-run, instead of freezing the last value.
- **Connector status is found, not guessed**: the EV charger's connector-status sensor is now resolved through the OCPP integration's own device and sensor registry instead of a guessed entity name. Chargers whose status sensor was renamed, and multi-connector chargers (where the status sensor belongs to a connector rather than the charge point), are read correctly - previously they looked permanently unplugged. Existing chargers are fixed on upgrade, with nothing to reconfigure.
- **Shared mode no longer freezes on a full phase**: in Shared distribution, a charger sitting on a phase with no headroom left used to stop the entire split - every other charger froze at whatever it had, even with amps free on their own phases. Chargers that physically can't receive now simply sit the round out while the rest fill up.
- **Shared mode no longer slows grid chargers by solar scarcity**: the split's solar-overshoot protection throttled *every* charger - including Standard (grid-backed) ones - when solar ran short, and switched itself off entirely in some mixed setups (letting two Solar Only chargers jointly overshoot the actual surplus and pull grid power). Solar- and Excess-bound chargers are now scaled against their own pool only, exactly to the available surplus; grid-backed chargers run unthrottled.
- **Household reading no longer collapses when a car ramps up**: on hybrid (series) inverters the household consumption is derived as inverter output minus charger draw - and the charger's OCPP reading reacts within seconds while the inverter's Modbus reading lags. During that lag the derived household briefly read 0 W and the engine handed the phantom headroom to the loads. The household value now bridges such dips for ~15 s (fast to rise, slow to fall), so a sensor lag can no longer cause a transient over-allocation.
- **Chargers reporting a total current are no longer triple-booked**: a charger whose *Current Import* sensor reports the sum across phases (instead of per-phase values) was booked at up to three times its real draw - the feedback loop then fabricated export, and the phantom surplus could over-allocate other loads. The total reading is now sanity-clamped the same way attribute-based readings already were.
- **Sliders keep to their lane**: a Min/Max Current slider value restored from before a reconfigure is now clamped into the new range, and moving one slider past its partner clamps at the partner's value instead of creating an inverted Min > Max range.
- **Charger auto-reset escalation restored**: The OCPP profile-reset → hard-reset escalation was crashing on a missing import and never ran. Fixed.
- **Grace-period status restored**: The charger status sensor crashed during the solar/excess grace period. Fixed - it now shows the grace countdown.
- **Configured timings now applied**: The site update frequency, solar grace period, and charge pause duration were silently ignored (always using defaults). They are now applied as configured.
- **More reliable startup**: A charger set up before its hub could stay permanently broken; it now retries. Also removed a leaked polling timer when the site update frequency changes, and an extra hub reload on startup.
- **OCPP command reliability**: OCPP charge-rate commands that failed to dispatch were wrongly recorded as sent, which could trigger spurious resets. Fixed.
- **Compliance check vs ramping**: A charger legitimately ramping up or down is no longer wrongly flagged as non-compliant.
- **DST-safe timers**: Pause and grace timers no longer jump by an hour across a daylight-saving transition.
- **Duplicate entity IDs prevented**: The config flow now rejects an entity ID already used by another Load Juggler device (previously a second smart load could silently lose its entities).
- **Circuit groups on partially-metered sites**: A 3-phase load in a circuit group is no longer wrongly capped to zero when the site meters only some phases.
- **Service input validation**: The `set_min_current` / `set_max_current` services now reject a value that would make the minimum exceed the maximum.
- **Fewer false notifications**: Phase-mismatch auto-detect notifications no longer re-fire repeatedly on noisy sites.
- **Robustness**: An invalid phase configuration value no longer crashes the power calculation.
- **Off-grid hub no longer stuck "Initializing"**: A solar entity that was unavailable at startup (e.g. a fresh restart at night) crashed the hub calculation, leaving the hub permanently in "Initializing". Fixed.
- **Hub updates continuously with no loads**: A hub with no loads configured ran its calculation only once and then showed stale values. It now recalculates every scan cycle.
- **Off-grid Site Remaining Power**: On a hub with no grid CTs, Site Remaining Power was clamped to 0 W even with battery and solar available. It now correctly reports grid headroom plus inverter-sourced (solar + battery) power.
- **Inverter capacity honored in headroom**: Site Remaining Power and Battery Remaining Power now subtract the power the inverter is *already* delivering to the household, and are capped by the inverter's rated capacity - previously a 4 kW inverter already supplying 1 kW still reported its full rating as available. The current output is taken from the configured inverter output sensors when present (correct for any wiring, including AC-coupled inverters and cascaded setups, where a genuinely negative reading now counts as power flowing *into* the inverter instead of being mistaken for production); without output sensors, a per-inverter topology-aware estimate is used - an AC-coupled inverter's battery charging no longer counts as reduced output.
- **Remaining Current A/B/C includes the inverter**: The per-phase remaining-current sensors now report total remaining current per phase (grid headroom + inverter share) and sum to Site Remaining Power ÷ voltage - previously they showed only grid breaker headroom and could contradict Site Remaining Power.
- **Off-grid phase count fixed**: An off-grid site forced all three grid phases to 0, making a 1- or 2-phase site look 3-phase - which split per-phase figures (e.g. Remaining Current A) across phantom phases. The site phase count is now the combination of the configured grid CT and inverter output sensors.
- **Smart plug stuck off fixed**: A plug that was currently off reported its connector status as "Available", which the engine treated as "idle" and excluded from power distribution - so an off plug was never allocated power and could never turn on. Plugs and hot water tanks (which have no connector) are now always eligible for allocation; only EVSEs require a connected car.
- **Hot water tank Solar Only mode fixed**: A tank in Solar Only mode was forbidden from heating whenever battery SOC was below the target, which overrode the tank's own setpoint logic - the away (below-minimum) and normal (below-target) setpoints never took effect. A tank now always heats to the setpoint chosen by its mode; Solar Only correctly heats to the away setpoint below minimum SOC, normal up to target, and boost above.
- **Managed power counts plugs and tanks**: The Current Managed Power sensor only summed EVSE draw - smart plugs and hot water tanks were never given per-phase currents, so they contributed 0 even with power metering configured. Plug and tank draw is now populated (from the power-monitor entity, or the set/element power when on), so it shows in Current Managed Power and is correctly subtracted from household consumption by the feedback loop.
- **No current smoothing on binary loads**: Smart plugs and hot water tanks are on/off loads, but their allocated current was run through the EVSE ramp/deadband smoothing - producing meaningless intermediate values and delaying the off transition. Binary loads now use the engine's target directly; only EVSEs are smoothed.
- **OCPP hard reset fixed**: The hard-reset escalation looked up the reset button by the Load Juggler entity ID instead of the OCPP charger ID, so it never found the button and silently fell back to a profile reset. A stuck charger can now actually be hard-reset.
- **`set_operating_mode` service accepts tank modes**: The service schema rejected the hot water tank modes ("Normal", "Freeze Protection"); they can now be set via the service.
- **Config flow: edited OCPP device ID kept**: Editing the auto-detected OCPP device ID during charger setup was silently discarded and the detected value used instead. The edit is now honored.
- **Status sensor names the missing input**: When a required sensor (solar, battery, grid, inverter output) is unavailable, the hub Status sensor now states exactly which input is needed instead of failing silently.
- **1-phase cars on watt-reporting chargers no longer reset-loop**: On a 3-phase EVSE that takes its limit in watts, a 1-phase car was checked for compliance using the charger's hardware phase count instead of the car's active phases - so a correctly-followed 16 A limit read back as ~5 A and triggered a perpetual non-compliance auto-reset loop. The compliance check now uses the same phase count the limit was sent with.
- **Zero site voltage no longer crashes the calculation**: A site phase voltage configured as 0 (or negative) caused a division-by-zero in the power calculation. It now falls back to 230 V with a warning.

## 2.0.4

**BREAKING**: Existing 1.x users must **remove and re-add** the integration.

### New Features

- **Per-load operating modes**: each charger/load has its own operating mode instead of a site-wide setting. EVSE modes: Standard, Solar Priority, Solar Only, Excess. Smart Load modes: Continuous, Solar Only, Excess.
- **Mixed-mode operation**: run different chargers in different modes simultaneously (e.g., one charger in Standard while another waits for Solar Only).
- **Multi-charger support** with 4 distribution modes (Shared, Priority, Optimized, Strict).
- **Smart Load support**: non-OCPP controllable loads (e.g., granny charger behind a Shelly smart plug) with binary on/off control, configurable power rating, and phase assignment.
- **Asymmetric inverter support**: flexible power pool across phases.
- **Battery integration**: SOC thresholds, charge/discharge power limits, battery-aware solar derivation.
- **2-phase OBC charger support** (e.g., VW eGolf, eUp, ID.3 base).
- **Charger phase mapping (L1/L2/L3 → A/B/C)** with per-phase constraint enforcement.
- **Power-based charging**: send watts instead of amps via OCPP charge rate unit auto-detection.
- **Per-phase inverter output entities**: optional sensors for each inverter phase with parallel/series wiring topology.
- **Entity auto-detection**: battery, solar, inverter, power monitoring, and wiring topology usually get auto detected.
- **Max import power limiter**: cap grid import independently of breaker rating.
- **HA service actions**: `set_operating_mode`, `set_distribution_mode`, `set_max_current`, `set_min_current`.
- **Available current display** for idle chargers.
- **EVSE charging status sensor** with mode-aware status messages (Battery Priority, Insufficient Solar, No Excess, etc.).
- **Hub status sensor** - shows site configuration health (OK, Initializing, No power measurement, Grid sensors unavailable) with detailed warnings as attributes.
- **Solar/Excess grace period**: configurable hold-at-minimum timer before pausing when conditions drop, preventing rapid on/off cycling.
- **Auto-detect grid CT inversion**: correlates charger draw vs grid current direction, fires persistent notification after repeated inverted readings.
- **Auto-detect phase mapping**: correlates charger draw vs per-phase grid deltas, notifies on wiring mismatch (opt-in).
- **Auto-detect OCPP meter sample interval**: uses charger's `MeterValueSampleInterval` as default update frequency.
- **Auto-detect smart plug power monitor**: discovers power sensors for Shelly, Sonoff, Tasmota, Kasa, Tuya plugs.
- **Circuit groups**: shared breaker limits for co-located loads. Group loads under a sub-breaker with a per-phase current limit. Post-distribution enforcement ensures combined allocation never exceeds the circuit limit.
- **Off-grid support**: grid CT entities are now optional. When no grid CTs are configured, the system infers active phases from inverter output entities and treats grid current as 0A. Solar production is derived from inverter output using a unified formula that works for both grid and off-grid sites.

### Resilience Improvements

- **Grid CT stale detection**: when configured grid CT sensors become unavailable, the system holds the last known EMA value. After 60s of continuous unavailability, all chargers fall to minimum current. Recovery is automatic with a log message.
- **Sensor unavailability handling**: `_UNAVAILABLE` sentinel pattern - solar, battery, and inverter sensors automatically hold their last EMA value during brief unavailability instead of decaying to 0.
- **OCPP/switch error handling**: `set_charge_rate` and plug switch commands wrapped in try-except to prevent update cycle crashes if the OCPP integration restarts.
- **Input validation**: NaN/Inf guard in EMA smoothing, voltage ≤0 fallback to 230V, plug empty-phase crash fix, stale circuit group member filtering.

### Bug Fixes

- Fixed charging instability from feedback loop oscillation (EMA smoothing + Schmitt trigger dead band + dual-frequency updates).
- Fixed grid CT feedback loop overcorrection when charger reports inflated per-phase draws (per-phase draw clamping).
- Fixed battery power sensor false-positive detection matching phone batteries.
- Fixed entity state lookup using shared `hass.data` store instead of entity ID guessing.
- Fixed battery SOC target/min sliders not feeding into calculation engine.
- Fixed EVSE min/max current sliders missing value clamping.
- Fixed connector status "Finishing"/"Faulted" treated as active - now correctly stops allocation and skips OCPP profiles.
- Fixed entity selector clearing (`suggested_value` instead of `default` so X button truly clears).
- Fixed options flow Submit → Next button on non-final steps.
- Fixed config entry not reloading on options change (stale battery sliders persisting).
- Fixed `total_site_available_power` and `available_grid_power` not capped by `max_grid_import_power`.

### UX Improvements
- Redesigned configuration flow with contextual help text, entity pickers, and number selectors.
- Phase mapping fields hidden on single-phase sites to prevent misconfiguration.
- Charger names auto-prettified from OCPP entity IDs.
- Phase B/C sensors hidden on single-phase sites.
