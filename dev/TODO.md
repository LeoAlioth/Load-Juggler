- [ ] **JUG-16 Shared battery bank behind parallel inverters: write control cannot reach a control-only entry** *(analysed 2026-09-07; no site has this topology yet - tackle it when one turns up)* - a 3-phase site built from parallel single-phase hybrids sharing one pack READS correctly: per-phase output sensors give `spans_phase` the right answer, `battery_power_total` sums only power-entity members, and configuring the pack on ONE inverter entry keeps `capacity_total` from being multiplied. The WRITE side cannot fan out, though: both `fleet.split_charge_limit` and the per-inverter SOC fan-out in `_compute_forecast_advice` gate on `m.has_battery and (m.capacity_kwh or 0) > 0`, so an entry declaring `battery_control` with no SOC/power entity of its own receives neither `forecast_charge_limit_w` nor `forecast_battery_max_soc` - Load Juggler throttles one inverter's register while its siblings keep pushing into the same pack. **Whether one write is enough is a hardware question, and the BMS is not the answer to it**: a CAN-connected BMS caps the pack's own charge current but cannot relay a setpoint WE chose - the entire point of the charge advice is holding the pack deliberately BELOW its capability to keep clipping headroom, which no BMS limit expresses. A single write suffices only where the units are parallel/three-phase GROUP-linked and the battery settings live on the master for the whole group (Deye/Sunsynk parallel, a Victron system under one GX/DVCC); independent units on a common DC bus each need their own. **How to settle it in two minutes on a real site**: command a reduced charge limit on one inverter, then read the siblings' registers back and watch the pack's charge power - if the siblings follow, it is group-linked and today's single write is already correct; if only that unit's share of the pack current drops, the fan-out needs building. That experiment is why this waits for a site instead of being fixed blind. **Fix direction**: gate the fan-out on DECLARED `battery_control` rather than on measured battery, and split the advice across the control-bearing members - headroom weighting has nothing to weigh when only one entry carries the capacity, so an equal split or the per-member charge cap as the weight is what a shared pack wants. Config notes for whoever hits this: every entry needs its output entity on its OWN phase (a blank `output` makes `spans_phase` true for all three, and a single-phase load then gets offered the whole fleet), asymmetric ticked on each member, the pack's own charge limit - not one inverter's - on the battery-bearing entry, and be aware that unequal inverter sizes lose power to `inverter_limits`' min-over-phases collapse.
- [ ] **JUG-17 Off-grid sites are structurally starved of forecast calibration - do NOT "fix" it by admitting curtailed intervals** *(established live on kozolec 2026-09-07; this entry exists to stop a future fix, not to request one)* - off-grid there is no export wall, so an array is demand-following: production always equals consumption plus charging. `battery_is_saturated` therefore reads curtailment off the pack, and once the pack is at its full-SOC every interval is excluded from the gain - correctly. Measured that day: pack 99 % against a 97 % full-SOC taking 57 W, AC consumption 3803 W, production 4263 W, forecast 6785 W - the array was throttled by ~3 kW and the exclusion was right. The consequence is that a site whose pack fills by mid-morning can only ever calibrate on its shoulders and its cloudy periods, so the gain moves slowly or not at all and `forecast_gain_days` may sit at 0 for a long time. **That is a true statement about what the site can know, not a defect.** Admitting curtailed intervals would discard exactly the evidence of an optimistic forecast (measured production is suppressed BY the quantity being measured), biasing the gain toward the forecast's own optimism - the same trap as calibrating on clipped kWh, noted under *Forecast calibration* below. The skipped share is published as the accuracy sensor's `gain_skipped_pct` so a starved observer says so rather than looking like a fresh restart. **If a faster off-grid gain is ever actually wanted**, it needs a source the site does not have today - a clear-sky reference, or one small unthrottled reference array - and that is a new input, not a tuning.

- [ ] **JUG-19 [FEATURE] Forecast calibration - apply what the observers measure** *(design settled 2026-08-31; the pure arithmetic and both observers are in, applying nothing yet)* - the plumbing that applied a correction (`scale_forecast_series`, `FleetMember.forecast_inflation`) was removed unused on 2026-10-12 (the ponytail audit); rebuild it with the apply step: bias each ARRAY before the merge and only for the clip integral, while `first_production_at` keeps reading the raw series (it carries its own 20% early bias, and stacking two margins on one number moves the overnight drop earlier for nothing). **Three pieces remain.** **(a) Wire the gain observer - DONE 2026-09-04**: per inverter, a 14-day series of 15-minute forecast/measured energy blocks (constrained intervals excluded per INTERVAL, not per day), gain = one energy-weighted ratio over the series, clamped ±25%, plus per-hour-of-day OFFSETS on that overall gain (`hourly_offsets`). Persisted in the *Forecast accuracy* sensor's `RestoreEntity` extra data (the integration's first persistent derived state); published as that sensor's `gain` / `gain_hourly` / `gain_days` attributes. **(b) Wire the peakiness observer**: per hub, replay the site's own ~2 s production samples through `clip_pair` over each completed 15-minute window and accumulate `true ÷ block-average` clip. No cloud model, no clear-sky envelope, no per-site knob - it is measured, not modelled. **(c) Then decide what to apply**, and remove the manual `inverter_forecast_inflation` field in the same change (keep the plumbing, drop only the source - a field that appears and disappears across releases is worse than either state). **What the measurement already says about peakiness**: the Jensen gap is real (clipping is convex and one-sided, so a block average can never overstate it) but SMALL at 15-minute resolution, which is what Open-Meteo publishes in its `watts` attribute. Modelled against a real day (2026-08-31, Deye array) with a concave-hull clear-sky proxy and a two-state sun/shade split, the correction came to a consistent **+5–7%** and did NOT grow when the export limit cut through the day's plateau rather than sitting at its peak - because the 15-minute series already expresses most of the cloud structure. Detection needs nothing but the series (sign-reversing block-to-block change, normalised by local level: that day read 0.00–0.04 through the clear morning and 0.50–0.68 through the broken-cloud afternoon); it is the MAGNITUDE that needed the hull. A ±25% gain clamp comfortably contains 6%, so the level bias is the one worth applying first. Note also that a local-maximum envelope is wrong on the ramps - it reported 42% cloudiness at 09:00 on a provably clear morning - which is why the hull, not a rolling max. **Do NOT calibrate on clipped kWh**, which was the obvious first idea: a reserve that works prevents the clipping you would measure, so the signal is destroyed by the control that consumes it, and curtailed energy is not measurable anyway - you cannot meter energy that did not happen. Original 2026-08-24 note follows: scale the remaining-day forecast series by the observed intra-day actual÷forecast ratio, so a sunny-side day fattens its own reserve in real time. That INTRA-day variant is a later phase than the slow per-array gain above - it chases a non-stationary quantity from a small early-morning sample, where the gain tracks a stationary one. Context: on marginal clipping days the reserve is small (≈1 kWh) while the forecast's clear-day tail error is ~5–10% of a 60 kWh day (observed 2026-08-24: forecast had the clip done by ~14:00, site still pinned at the wall 14:35). A week of forecast-vs-actual comparison says calibration is right (midday per-array forecast exact; daily optimism is cloud-model error), so Open-Meteo parameters are not the fix. The other two mechanisms once listed here are done: the reserve pad was superseded structurally by the destination-anchored reserve (the band between the normal SOC ceiling and 100% is the buffer, filled above the ceiling only by what Excess loads cannot absorb), and the base-consumption sensitivity is gone entirely - the interim reconstructed-export trim (2026-08-24) was superseded 2026-08-26 by the memoryless direct-feedback charge advice (battery power + reconstructed-export error), which never consults base at all, so the lowered-base workaround no longer drags the export equilibrium.
