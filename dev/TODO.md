- [ ] **JUG-10 [BUG] The wiring-topology labels describe the hardware, the arithmetic needs the position** *(2026-09-18, confirmed on a real site: a SolarEdge PV inverter on a Deye hybrid's LOAD port)* - `wiring_topology` is one per-member flag carrying two independent facts. The labels sell it as a hardware question - "Parallel (AC-coupled / no battery)" against "Series (Hybrid / battery)", and `inverter_config`'s description says "power flows through this inverter (hybrid with DC-coupled battery)" - but every consumer of it asks a POSITION question: `compute_household_per_phase` picks its formula by where the inverter's output lands, and `_battery_output_term` by where the battery sits relative to the conversion. A grid-tied PV inverter cabled to a hybrid's load port is *parallel by hardware* (AC-coupled, no battery of its own) and *series by position* (its output is inside the hybrid's output bus, behind everything the hybrid meters), so the labels walk the owner straight to the wrong answer. Picking "parallel" makes `mixed_topologies` true and hands that member to the parallel formula at `hub_calculation.py:249`, which credits its output against the grid bus it is not connected to; picking "series" is arithmetically right today - the signed netting in `output_power_measured` already cancels the parent's negative back-feed reading against the child's positive one, which is exactly what `fleet.py:535`'s comment describes - but nothing in the UI suggests a battery-less PV string inverter belongs there. **Minimum fix**: relabel and re-describe both options in terms of where the output lands ("feeds the same bus as the grid" / "feeds through another inverter or carries the loads behind it"), which costs nothing and is a strings-only change. **Proper fix**: replace the flag with an ATTACHMENT POINT per inverter - the node its AC output feeds, chosen from the nodes that already exist on the site (the grid/meter bus, another inverter's AC input, another inverter's load port) - and derive parallel/series from the position rather than asking for it. That also gives `mixed_topologies` something better than a boolean to reason about, and it is the same model Load Insights is adopting for its own inverter pages, so the two should agree on the vocabulary. **Plan lj-5** (`lj-5-household-refactor.md`, run order 10, the biggest, last): deletes `wiring_topology` and computes household = grid + sum(inverter output - input); migration step 9; decided 2026-10-12 to keep the *grid flows through this inverter* tick. - [ ] **JUG-11 [FEATURE] The household sum telescopes - wiring_topology is not needed to compute it** *(2026-09-18, derived while designing the same thing in Load Insights and verified against his home's ten days of history)* - `compute_household_per_phase` branches on topology and runs two different formulas, and `fleet_topology`/`mixed_topologies` exist to decide which. One formula covers every arrangement, including the mixed ones: **household = grid + Σ over inverters of (output − input)**. Each inverter contributes what it ADDS - a PV string inverter has no AC input so it contributes its whole output, a hybrid with the grid flowing through it contributes output minus input, so the grid it passed on is not counted twice. **It is arrangement-independent**, which is the part worth checking rather than believing: with a PV inverter on the main bus, `grid + (deye_out − deye_in) + se_out` = `loads_main + deye_out`; with the same PV inverter cabled to the hybrid's LOAD PORT, the identical expression = `loads_main + loads_backup`. Both correct, no flag consulted. Whatever sits between the utility CTs and a hybrid's own grid port - a non-backed-up panel - falls out of the same sum, because a hybrid measures its grid port itself and the difference against the external CTs is exactly that load. **Verified numerically**: against the owner's home (SolarEdge meter + inverter) the formula reproduced his hand-built per-phase consumption templates over 246,000 samples on three phases - median, p5 and p95 all 0 W. **What this does NOT retire**: `wiring_topology` still decides where a battery's conversion losses land (DC-coupled charges on the bus in front of the inverter, AC-coupled pays a conversion in and another out) and `_battery_output_term` still needs it to say how much of a battery's flow shows in the AC output. Detection needs nothing but the series (sign-reversing block-to-block change, normalised by local level: that day read 0.00–0.04 through the clear morning and 0.50–0.68 through the broken-cloud afternoon); it is the MAGNITUDE that needed the hull. A ±25% gain clamp comfortably contains 6%, so the level bias is the one worth applying first. Note also that a local-maximum envelope is wrong on the ramps - it reported 42% cloudiness at 09:00 on a provably clear morning - which is why the hull, not a rolling max. **Do NOT calibrate on clipped kWh**, which was the obvious first idea: a reserve that works prevents the clipping you would measure, so the signal is destroyed by the control that consumes it, and curtailed energy is not measurable anyway - you cannot meter energy that did not happen. Original 2026-08-24 note follows: scale the remaining-day forecast series by the observed intra-day actual÷forecast ratio, so a sunny-side day fattens its own reserve in real time. That INTRA-day variant is a later phase than the slow per-array gain above - it chases a non-stationary quantity from a small early-morning sample, where the gain tracks a stationary one. Context: on marginal clipping days the reserve is small (≈1 kWh) while the forecast's clear-day tail error is ~5–10% of a 60 kWh day (observed 2026-08-24: forecast had the clip done by ~14:00, site still pinned at the wall 14:35). A week of forecast-vs-actual comparison says calibration is right (midday per-array forecast exact; daily optimism is cloud-model error), so Open-Meteo parameters are not the fix. The other two mechanisms once listed here are done: the reserve pad was superseded structurally by the destination-anchored reserve (the band between the normal SOC ceiling and 100% is the buffer, filled above the ceiling only by what Excess loads cannot absorb), and the base-consumption sensitivity is gone entirely - the interim reconstructed-export trim (2026-08-24) was superseded 2026-08-26 by the memoryless direct-feedback charge advice (battery power + reconstructed-export error), which never consults base at all, so the lowered-base workaround no longer drags the export equilibrium.
